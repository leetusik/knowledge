Background — three leaves, one page

Most of this curriculum has walked load-bearing machinery: the chat turn (#5), retrieval (#7), ingestion (#8), the content agent (#13–#15), Celery (#16), the operator console (#17), billing (#18). Every one of those has other things standing on top of it, which is why each needed a page of its own.

The three subsystems here are the opposite. In graph terms they are leaves: no part of the product's main paths — a chat turn, a retrieval call, an ingestion run, a content draft, a subscription renewal — reads them, calls them, or waits on them. Pull any one of them out and the rest of changple5 keeps working exactly as before.

"Leaf", measured rather than asserted. Grepping the backend for inbound imports gives a short and honest list:

  • feedback — zero inbound imports. Nothing in the repo imports it.
  • mbti — one: accounts/usage.py counts a user's MBTISubmission rows for GET /api/auth/usage. A count, not a behaviour.
  • consultations — one read (accounts/operator_serializers.py counts a user's 상담신청 for the operator user-detail 활동 strip) and two writes into it (the consulting follow-up in conversations/views.py, and mbti's own lead endpoint). Writes point into a leaf; they do not make it a dependency.

Both count-reads use function-local imports, so even the module-load graph stays clean.

Their HTTP mount points, from apps/backend/config/urls.py:

path("api/mbti/",                  include("mbti.urls")),
path("api/consultation-requests/", include("consultations.urls")),
path("api/",                       include("feedback.urls")),   # -> /api/feedback, /api/admin/feedback…

The feedback app mounts at bare api/ on purpose: it owns both its user-facing capture route and its own /api/admin/feedback… operator routes, rather than leaking a Vocky client into the operators app (explainer #17).

And their sizes, counting non-test, non-migration Python:

AppLinesOf which
apps/backend/mbti~2,268metadata.py is 1,162 of them — result copy, not logic. The engine is 663 lines (typology + items + validation + scoring).

So why put them on one page instead of writing three short ones? Because read together they are a compact museum of how this repository does things:

  • mbti is a measurement model as pure Python. One module is declared the single source of truth for the typology, and every other module derives from it rather than restating it — to the point where the item count, the axis count and the display-name maps are all checked by assert statements that run at import time.
  • consultations is a record designed to outlive its source. It snapshots the text it was created from, because the thing that produced that text gets hard-deleted 30 days later.
  • feedback is a dormant-by-default external integration that exists twice — once as a REST proxy on Django, once as an MCP client inside the agent — with different credentials, different transports, and deliberately different contracts about how they are allowed to fail.

The rest of the page is three independent walks, in that order, followed by a short comparison that puts them side by side. Nothing here depends on anything else here, so you can read the three sections in any order you like.

Intuition — an instrument, a ledger, and a bridge

1. 창업가 MBTI is an instrument, and instruments must be reproducible

창업가 MBTI ("founder MBTI") is a 40-question quiz on the product's /mbti page. You answer each statement on a five-point 그렇다/아니다 scale, and you get back one of 16 founder types plus a "준비도" (readiness) reading.

The temptation with something like this is to ask a language model to judge the answers. This code does the opposite, and the module docstring says so out loud: the scorer is "deterministic, LLM-free … pure function of the answers". Two consequences follow, and they drive the whole design.

First: the same answers must always give the same type. A user who retakes the quiz identically must land on the identical type, and an operator must be able to re-derive an old result years later. That is why the raw submission is stored verbatim and the type is recomputed server-side on every submit rather than trusted from the client.

Second: the arithmetic must have no floating-point ambiguity. Each axis is a tug-of-war between two poles. Eight statements load on it; agreeing with a statement pulls toward the pole that statement points at. Sum everything up and you get a number; whichever side of the midpoint it lands on wins.

Here is the subtlety that makes this more than a sum. The neutral answer (보통이다) is the integer 3, and each item's two poles always sum to 6. So an "I felt nothing either way about any of these" profile lands on exactly the midpoint — not near it, on it. A float comparison (raw > 1.5 * n) would be answering a question about equality with a data type that is bad at equality. The code instead compares doubled integers, 2*raw against 6*n, and gives the exact tie its own named branch. We will watch it happen in the Code section with real numbers.

준비도 is not a fifth axis. Questions 33–40 ask whether you have already done concrete preparation (separated startup capital from operating capital, computed a break-even point, agreed roles with partners in writing). They are summed plainly, 8 to 40, and mapped to one of four bands. They never touch the typology. Mixing them in would have been the obvious shortcut and would have made the 16 types mean something different for a prepared founder than for an unprepared one.

2. A consultation request is a ledger entry that must outlive its source

상담신청 ("consultation request") is what happens when a visitor asks to be contacted. Four different surfaces can produce one: the end of a 컨설팅 session, the 무료진단 flow on the apex site, the plain /contact form, and — new in this page — the MBTI result screen.

The interesting design decision is not the four sources; it is what the row stores. A consultation raised at the end of a guest 진단 session refers to a report that lives in a ConversationSession. Those anonymous sessions are hard-deleted by a nightly Celery beat job 30 days later (explainer #3 for guests, #16 for the beat schedule). If the consultation row merely pointed at the session, the operator who opens it on day 31 would find an empty page.

Once you see the row as a ledger entry rather than a pointer, the rest of the app follows mechanically: one write seam so the notification email is wired at one chokepoint instead of four; a Celery task that mails a human-readable Korean summary; and an operator queue with three statuses (pending → contacted → closed).

3. Vocky is one service reached by two very different bridges

Vocky is an external feedback service (a separate product, at vocky.hi2vi.com). changple5 sends it feedback from two completely separate places, and the two paths share nothing but the destination:

The user-facing path is ordinary: a browser posts, Django validates and forwards, the user gets a receipt. The agent path is the interesting one, because it runs inside a chat turn. If the model decides "the user just complained about search being slow, record that", the recording must not be able to hurt the answer the user is waiting for. That single constraint produces all three of the properties the agent module owns — dormant when unconfigured, never raises, bounded by a timeout — and it explains why the model receives the identical acknowledgment whether the capture succeeded, failed, or was never even attempted.

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is a wire protocol for exposing tools to language models: a client connects, calls initialize, and can then list tools (tools/list) or invoke one (tools/call). changple5's agent skips the listing step entirely — more on why below.

typology.py — one module declared the source of truth

Its docstring is unusually direct about its job: it is "the single source of truth for the typology that every other mbti module reads". Four bipolar axes, each with two poles, and the pole's machine key is its code letter:

The Likert scale lives here too, and the comment explains why it lives here rather than on each item: it is a property of the measurement model, not of any one question. One constant deserves special attention:

LIKERT_MIN = 1
LIKERT_MAX = 5
# Reversal constant: a reverse-scored item contributes ``LIKERT_REVERSE - v``
# (agreeType = v, disagreeType = 6 − v; the two poles always sum to 6 per item).
LIKERT_REVERSE = LIKERT_MIN + LIKERT_MAX  # == 6

Reverse-keyed items. Half the statements on each axis are written from the other pole's point of view — "아무리 끌리는 아이템이어도 손익분기점이 보이지 않으면 시작할 마음이 거의 생기지 않는다" argues for 검증형 C, not 감각형 G. A 5 on that statement has to become a 1 in favour of G. That is what 6 − v does: it mirrors the answer around the neutral point, and it stays inside {1,2,3,4,5} because the scale is symmetric. Psychometric questionnaires mix directions like this so that a respondent who simply agrees with everything does not automatically land at one extreme.

items.py — a static bank that checks itself at import

There is no items table and no migration: the 40 questions are Python literals. Each is built by one of two tiny helpers — _p(id, axis, direction, text) for a personality item, _r(id, text) for a readiness item — which exist mainly so the 40 declarations read as a table rather than as forty constructor calls:

_p("q1", AXIS_1, POLE_G, "숫자 계산이 아직 덜 되어 있어도, 고객이 좋아할 “느낌”이 강하면 먼저 시도해보고 싶다."),
…
_p("q5", AXIS_1, POLE_C, "아무리 끌리는 아이템이어도 손익분기점이 보이지 않으면 시작할 마음이 거의 생기지 않는다."),
…
_r("q35", "고정비, 변동비, 마진을 넣어 손익분기점을 계산해봤다."),

direction is "the pole that agreement scores toward" — that is the entire content of a personality item beyond its text. Readiness items carry axis=None, direction=None. Ids q1–q40 map 1:1 onto the spec's question numbers, which makes the bank reviewable by the non-programmer who wrote the questions.

Then the module asserts its own shape at import time — meaning a malformed bank crashes the container at startup rather than producing quietly wrong types in production:

The client-facing payload is built by build_items_payload(), and what it omits is the point: {"id", "text", "kind"} per item, plus the scale and one guidance string — and no axis, direction or pole. Telling the browser which pole a statement scores toward would both leak the instrument and let a user farm a desired result.

validation.py — the one place untrusted input is bounded

96 lines standing between a JSON body and the scorer. The expected count is derived, not typed:

EXPECTED_ANSWER_COUNT = len(MBTI_ITEM_IDS)

and the value check is stricter than it first looks:

def _coerce_likert_int(raw_value: object, *, answer_id: str) -> int:
    if isinstance(raw_value, bool) or not isinstance(raw_value, int):
        raise MbtiAnswerValidationError(…)
    if raw_value not in LIKERT_VALUE_SET:
        raise MbtiAnswerValidationError(…)
    return raw_value

isinstance(raw_value, bool) is checked first because in Python isinstance(True, int) is True — True would otherwise sail through as the integer 1. (Explainer #3 found the same guard in the agent's JWT parsing; it is a house habit.) A string "3", a float 3.0, a 0 and a 6 are all rejected as well. Finally, after the exact-count, no-duplicate and all-known-id checks have already made it impossible, the function asserts the set identity anyway — the comment calls it "cheap insurance".

scoring.py — the doubled-integer decision, measured

The scorer walks the validated answers, skips readiness items, and accumulates each contribution toward its axis's positive pole:

toward_positive = item.direction == axis.positive
contribution = value if toward_positive else LIKERT_REVERSE - value
raw_by_axis[item.axis] += contribution
n_by_axis[item.axis] += 1

and then decides each axis:

def _axis_pole(raw_sum: int, n: int, axis_id: AxisId) -> str:
    axis = AXES[axis_id]
    doubled_sum = 2 * raw_sum
    midpoint = LIKERT_REVERSE * n        # == 2 * (3n)
    if doubled_sum > midpoint:
        return axis.positive
    if doubled_sum < midpoint:
        return axis.negative
    return axis.tie_break                # the exact midpoint raw == 3n

Note that n is counted from the bank at score time rather than hard-coded, so re-balancing the questionnaire needs no change here.

The whole mbti domain is import-clean — typology, items, validation and scoring touch neither Django nor the database — so we can run the real scorer directly and watch it decide. Two profiles, both produced by executing the shipped code:

# Profile A — every one of the 40 answers is 3 (보통이다)

readiness = 24 → band "low"

# Profile B — a leaning founder
#   a1: 5 on the four G items, 2 on the four C items
#   a2: 2 on the four E items, 4 on the four S items
#   a3: 4 on all eight items          <-- deliberately balanced
#   a4: 3 on the four I items, 4 on the four F items

Profile A is the all-neutral run the docstring predicts: every axis lands on raw = 3n = 24, every tie-break fires, and the user gets GEBI — the A-side corner of the cube. Profile B is more instructive. Axis a1 is a landslide: four items at 5 pointing at G (+5 each = 20) and four items at 2 pointing at C (6 − 2 = 4 each = 16), total 36 out of a possible 48 → 75% 감각형. Axis a3 is the interesting one: answering 4 to every statement, half of which argue for the opposite pole, produces 4 × 4 + (6−4) × 4 = 24 — dead centre, again resolved deterministically to B.

Why determinism matters more than "fairness" at the tie. A random or timestamp-based tie-break would mean the same answers give different types on different days, and the stored submission could no longer be re-scored to the stored result. The code chooses a fixed winner (the A-side pole), documents it in three places, and accepts the small bias that follows.

Readiness is a plain sum with four bands, the thresholds sitting as named module constants: high ≥ 33, medium ≥ 25, low ≥ 17, else very_low — which is why 33 and 32 land in different bands above.

models.py — one table, shaped for a typology that may change

class MBTISubmission(TimeStampedModel):
    user             = ForeignKey(AUTH_USER_MODEL, on_delete=CASCADE, related_name="mbti_submissions")
    answers          = JSONField(default=dict)      # the canonical [{id, value}, …] — reproducible rescore
    result_type      = CharField(max_length=64, db_index=True)

        ordering = ("-created_at",)

Two decisions worth copying. axis_scores is JSON rather than four integer columns "so a later typology change (more/fewer axes) needs no schema migration". And readiness_score is a dedicated nullable column rather than another key inside axis_scores, because the views iterate that map per axis and a stray key would break the loop. Legacy rows are null, and the view recomputes from the stored answers when it finds one — which is only possible because the raw answers were kept.

The wall is deliberately placed between taking the quiz and seeing the result: anyone can fetch the items, only a logged-in user can submit. Three details in this file repay reading:

The partitioned shuffle. MbtiItemsView splits the payload by kind, shuffles the 32 personality items among themselves and the 8 readiness items among themselves, and always emits personality-then-readiness. The bank is grouped by axis, and serving it in that order would telegraph the grouping; the 준비도 block has to stay last because it is a labeled section with its own instructions ("'그럴 것 같다'가 아니라 '이미 해두었는지'를 기준으로"). Order is cosmetic — the scorer keys on ids — and the static bank is never mutated: fresh copies are shuffled.

The legacy-row guard. /me returns 404 not only when the user has never submitted, but also when the stored result_type is not one of the current 16 — a row left by an earlier eight-type version of this quiz (keys like execute_creative_org). Dropping the user back onto the quiz is a better answer than a 500 from a KeyError in the metadata map.

Percents, never raw sums. The "scores" overlay that /submit and /me attach is built by submission_scores_payload, which converts each axis's stored raw into a complementary percentage pair via axis_percent(raw, n) → (pos, 100 − pos) and resolves the Korean labels through AXIS_NAMES / POLE_NAMES. The internal score stays server-side; the shareable /types/<id> DTO carries no overlay at all, because there is no submission behind it.

Walk 2 — Consultation requests: apps/backend/consultations/

919 lines, and the whole app is one idea repeated: four funnels, one row shape, one write seam, one email.

models.py — the snapshot decision, in the docstring

class ConsultationRequest(TimeStampedModel):
    """Unified 상담신청 record aggregating every submission source.

    The 내용 is SNAPSHOTTED (``content_snapshot`` + inert ``cited_documents``) so an
    operator can still follow up after the 30-day guest purge hard-deletes the linked
    session; the session FK is therefore ``SET_NULL`` and the snapshot is the durable
    record.
    """
    class Source(TextChoices):  CONSULTING | DIAGNOSIS(무료진단) | CONTACT | MBTI
    class Status(TextChoices):  PENDING | CONTACTED | CLOSED

Four fields carry the payload: content_snapshot (the report or 진단 결과 text as it read at submit time — empty for /contact, which produces no report), additional_notes (the user's free text, plus whatever each funnel folds in), cited_documents (a JSON list of {post_id, title, source_url}, described in the help text as "inert … never an FK into conversations"), and the nullable session link.

Why "inert" is the right word for cited_documents. The citations could have been foreign keys into the documents table. Then a re-crawl that changes a post's title, or a purge that removes it, would silently rewrite history in a record the operator is using to call a customer back. A denormalised copy is worse for querying and better for testimony — and testimony is what this row is.

Two fields are newer (P76) and worth a careful reading, because they contradict a plausible assumption about this codebase:

client_ip = models.GenericIPAddressField(null=True, blank=True, help_text=(
    "Submitter's IP as reported by the edge (Cloudflare -> edge nginx -> app: the first "
    "X-Forwarded-For hop …), stored in CLEAR as evidence for abusive submissions - never "
    "hashed, truncated, or used for authorization. …  NULL = not captured. "
    "Retention: lives and dies with this row."))
client_user_agent = models.CharField(max_length=512, null=True, blank=True, …)

changple5 stores no IP addresses for chat, conversations or accounts. It stores one here, in clear, for exactly one purpose — evidence about an abusive submission — and the help text pins down all three things that usually go unsaid: where the value comes from, what it may never be used for (authorization), and how long it lives (as long as the row). The UA is capped at 512 characters defensively; the IP is never transformed, because a truncated or hashed IP is evidence you cannot act on.

The second model is four fields and a docstring:

class NotificationRecipient(TimeStampedModel):
    email  = EmailField(unique=True)
    active = BooleanField(default=True, db_index=True)
    # "Delete is SOFT (``active=False``) so the unique email is preserved; re-registering
    #  a removed email reactivates the existing row instead of colliding on the unique key."

A unique column plus a hard delete is a classic small trap: remove a recipient, re-add them a month later, and the insert collides with a row you thought was gone. The soft flag plus update_or_create in the view makes re-adding idempotent.

services.py — the seam, and a trust rule stated once

record_consultation_submission() is a keyword-only function that creates the row and then fires the notification. Its docstring names its own reason for existing: "the consulting dual-write, the service-token ingress, and the async notification email all create rows here, so the email trigger is wired once at one chokepoint instead of at three call sites."

def _enqueue_notification(consultation_id: int) -> None:
    try:
        from .tasks import send_consultation_notification
        send_consultation_notification.delay(consultation_id)
    except Exception:  # noqa: BLE001 - enqueue failure must not fail the submit
        logger.exception("failed to enqueue consultation %s notification", consultation_id)

Two things here connect to explainer #16. First, this is the .delay() idiom with a function-local import (to avoid an import cycle), not the repository's usual transaction.on_commit(...) dispatch — and that is correct rather than sloppy, because settings.py sets no ATOMIC_REQUESTS: Django is in autocommit, so the row is already committed when the task is queued and the worker cannot outrun it. Second, the try/except is the house "fire-and-forget" contract: a Redis outage must not turn a customer's 상담신청 into an error page.

capture_client_meta(request) reuses the guest-limit trust rule wholesale — first X-Forwarded-For hop under GUEST_TRUST_FORWARDED_FOR, else REMOTE_ADDR — and its docstring carries a prohibition that is really a piece of architectural reasoning:

"""…
**Never call this on the service-token ingress**: there ``request`` describes the
changple_web BFF host, and storing that as "the visitor" would look like an answer
while being none. That path reads the payload instead.
"""

"Would look like an answer while being none" is the whole argument for why the ingress reads the IP from the request body instead. A wrong IP is worse than a null one, because nobody audits a column that is always populated.

tasks.py — the one Celery task in the app

send_consultation_notification is the entire async surface of consultations (explainer #16 counted 58 registered tasks across the backend; this is one of them). It does four things in order: load the row, read the recipients, build a Korean body, send. Every step has a named non-failure:

consultation = ConsultationRequest.objects.filter(pk=consultation_id).first()
if consultation is None:
    return {"status": "missing", …}

recipients = list(NotificationRecipient.objects.filter(active=True).values_list("email", flat=True))
if not recipients:
    return {"status": "no_recipients", …}

try:
    mail.send_mail(subject=f"[창플] 새 상담신청 ({source_label})", message=body,
                   from_email=settings.CONSULTATION_FROM_EMAIL,
                   recipient_list=recipients, fail_silently=False)
except Exception:  # noqa: BLE001 - a notification failure must never crash
    return {"status": "error", …}

return {"status": "sent", "consultation_id": …, "recipients": len(recipients)}

The body is assembled as labeled Korean sections — ■ 신청자, ■ 내용 (only when a snapshot exists), ■ 추가 내용, ■ 참고할만한 글, and finally ■ 상담신청 상세 with a deep link built from settings.OPERATOR_BASE_URL. It is written for a person to act on, not for a machine to parse.

Three mail paths, three different designs — and each one is right. Put them side by side:

  • consultations (here): a real Celery task, recipients read from the ORM inside the task. A product notification. If the database is broken there is no consultation to notify about, so depending on the ORM costs nothing.
  • ingestion/alerts.py (#16): deliberately ORM-free, with its recipient module kept model-free so the ORM never enters that import graph. An outage alert has to work while things are broken — including while the database is the broken thing.
  • billing (#18): no task at all — a local transaction.on_commit closure around send_mail, recipients from an env variable, because the renewal sweep already runs inside a worker and queueing a task from a task would buy nothing.

The shared thing across all three is the pattern — fire-and-forget, log-and-swallow, never fail the caller — and not a line of shared code. A "unified mail helper" would have had to be ORM-free, task-based and closure-based simultaneously.

views.py and admin_views.py — one ingress, four operator routes

ConsultationIngestView (POST /api/consultation-requests/ingest) is the door for the apex site's BFF. Its authentication list mirrors the guest-capable ordering explainer #3 explained:

    if not isinstance(request.user, AgentServicePrincipal):
        raise PermissionDenied("Consultation ingress is restricted to the agent service token.")

Read the two together and the three outcomes are deliberate: a valid first-party JWT authenticates and is then rejected with 403; a missing or bad token makes the service authenticator raise, giving 401; only the service principal writes. The serializer restricts source to diagnosis and contact — the other two sources are produced in-process and can never arrive over this door.

For a diagnosis submission the view resolves the report through a single import from the other app:

    return Response({"detail": "Guest diagnosis session not found."}, status=404)

The comment calls this a "read-direction inversion": rather than consultations reaching into the guest hard-scope and message internals (three imports), conversations exposes one function that returns exactly what the snapshot needs. Explainer #4 owns those internals.

The operator side is four routes behind a locally declared mixin:

    # Mixed-auth admin gate, mirroring each app's local copy …
    authentication_classes = [JWTAuthentication, SessionAuthentication]
    permission_classes = [IsAuthenticated, IsOperatorUser]

Bearer token for the operator SPA, Django admin session for a browser tab, and IsOperatorUser (user_type == "admin") deciding. Explainer #17 measured the pattern across the backend: eight apps import that one permission class, and each declares its own two-line copy of the mixin rather than importing another app's — feedback's copy, in the next walk, even names this one as its mirror in a comment. The shared thing is the permission class; the mixin is duplicated on purpose so no app depends on another's admin module.

One route is worth pausing on. DELETE /admin/requests/<pk> is a hard delete, and the docstring justifies it precisely:

"""…``delete`` is a HARD delete of the single row (P55.S4): nothing points at a
``ConsultationRequest`` (no inbound FK anywhere), its outbound ``session`` is
``SET_NULL`` and ``cited_documents`` is an inert JSON snapshot, so the row is
self-contained and removing it orphans nothing. It is also IRREVERSIBLE and
unlogged — this app has no audit trail …"""

"Self-contained" is exactly the property the snapshot design bought, and here it is being spent: because the row copies rather than references, it can be deleted without a cascade analysis. Recipients, by contrast, are soft-deleted — because their uniqueness constraint has to keep remembering them.

3a. Backend: feedback/client.py (346 lines)

The first thing to notice about this app is a file that is not there: there is no models.py and no migrations/. The 피드백 console reads Vocky live; changple5 keeps no local copy of a feedback record. That is why "delete" in the console is irreversible — there is nothing local to restore from.

The client is deliberately unfashionable: urllib from the standard library, no requests, no httpx, no retry library. Three properties are worth stealing.

Dormant before the network, not after it.

def _require_vocky_config(*, unavailable_detail: str) -> tuple[str, str]:
    base_url   = str(getattr(settings, "VOCKY_BASE_URL", "") or "").strip()
    auth_token = str(getattr(settings, "VOCKY_API_AUTH_TOKEN", "") or "").strip()
    if not base_url or not auth_token:
        raise VockyServiceUnavailableError(unavailable_detail)
    return base_url, auth_token

Every call starts here, so an environment that has not configured Vocky answers 503 without ever opening a socket. A blank token is treated exactly like a blank URL, with the reasoning spelled out in the docstring: Vocky's auth is mandatory, so a blank token could only ever produce a 401 — pretending it might work would just convert a configuration mistake into a slow network error.

An explicit User-Agent, learned the hard way.

# urllib's default `Python-urllib/x.y` UA is banned by the Cloudflare in front
# of the public Vocky app (error 1010); identify explicitly on every call.
VOCKY_USER_AGENT = "changple5-feedback-proxy/1.0"

This is the kind of fact that costs an afternoon once and a comment forever. The agent-side module later cites this same lesson when it sets its own UA, even though httpx's default is a different string that is probably fine — the docstring reasons that the question is cheaper to kill than to keep asking.

No retries, and a status map that means something. The error classes each carry the HTTP status the view will return:

ExceptionStatusRaised when
VockyServiceUnavailableError503unconfigured (no network call), or a timeout / connection failure
VockyBadGatewayError502Vocky rejected the request, or returned something unreadable
VockyNotFoundError404no such record in this credential's scope

Why an out-of-scope id is a 404 and never a 403. The project scope comes from the credential, not from a parameter in the request. So when the operator console asks for a feedback id belonging to a different project, Vocky's honest answer is "no such record here" — it has no way to say "that exists but is not yours" without leaking that it exists. The client passes that 404 through deliberately (the docstring: "a deleted or out-of-scope feedback id is a client-visible fact about the record, not a gateway failure"), while an upstream 401/403 — which means our own key is wrong or revoked — becomes a 502 with a message naming it as an operator-side misconfiguration. The key itself is never echoed.

The write path validates the receipt before claiming success:

if response_status != 202:
    raise VockyBadGatewayError()
…
def _validate_receipt(raw_receipt):          # request_id non-blank str
    …                                        # accepted_at parses as ISO-8601

A 200 that is not a 202, a receipt without a request_id, a timestamp that will not parse — all of them mean "we cannot honestly tell the user this was recorded", so they become 502s. The read path (added later, for the operator console) reuses the same request helper against Vocky's project-scoped review API: list_vocky_feedback, get_vocky_feedback, create_vocky_feedback_comment, delete_vocky_feedback.

3b. Backend: feedback/views.py — what actually gets sent

FeedbackCaptureView (POST /api/feedback, IsAuthenticated) serves two shapes, matching the two places the UI offers feedback: a 👍/👎 on an assistant message, and a free-text form in the profile modal (target_id: "profile-modal-feedback").

The message shape is ownership-scoped, and the checks are worth reading as a pair:

session = get_object_or_404(owner_conversation_session_queryset(user=user), nonce=…)
message = get_object_or_404(ConversationMessage.objects.filter(session=session), public_id=…)
if message.role != ConversationMessage.Role.ASSISTANT:
    raise ValidationError({"target_id": ["Message feedback is only valid for assistant messages."]})

Someone else's conversation is a 404 (not a 403 — same reasoning as Vocky's own 404 above: do not confirm that an id exists), and rating your own message is a 400. The surface shape has no ownership scope at all, because there is nothing to own.

What makes the payload useful to whoever reads it later is the context it carries. A thumbs-down on an answer, with no idea what the question was, is nearly worthless:

MAX_TARGET_TEXT_LENGTH  = 4000     # the rated message itself
MAX_CONTEXT_TEXT_LENGTH = 1200     # each surrounding message
CONTEXT_BEFORE_COUNT    = 2
CONTEXT_AFTER_COUNT     = 1

So Vocky receives the rated message (up to 4,000 chars) plus a four-message window — two before, the target, one after — each truncated to 1,200 characters with an ellipsis, ordered chronologically. The window query orders by (created_at, id) in both directions, because two messages written in the same instant still need a stable order. Alongside it rides used_context: the citations attached to that answer, read from the ConversationMessageCitedDocument snapshot rows (explainer #4) — so the reader of a complaint can see which 창플 카페 posts the model was working from. A small helper, _compact_metadata, drops None values so optional fields are absent rather than null.

3c. Backend: feedback/admin_views.py — a proxy that adds exactly one thing

The operator console's 피드백 screen is a passthrough of Vocky's own shapes, and the module docstring lists its self-imposed limits: the keyset cursor stays a keyset cursor (next_cursor, no page numbers and no total, because Vocky has none), and no search, sort or triage parameter is invented anywhere. Proxies that "improve" an upstream API tend to end up implementing it.

The single enrichment is identity. Vocky stores user_id as free-form text; changple5 writes str(user.pk) into it. So the proxy joins those strings back to local users:

MAX_USER_PK = 2**63 - 1        # changple5 PKs are BigAutoField

def _coerce_user_pk(raw):
    if isinstance(raw, bool): return None          # bool-before-int, again
    …
    return value if 1 <= value <= MAX_USER_PK else None

users_by_pk = User.objects.filter(pk__in=list(wanted)).only(…).in_bulk() if wanted else {}

One bulk query per response, never an N+1; an unknown, non-numeric or out-of-range id yields user: null and the console falls back to showing the raw string. The range check is not pedantry — an integer larger than a 64-bit column is a database driver error, not a lookup miss, so it must be stopped before the query.

3d. Agent: chat/vocky_feedback.py (562 lines) — three owned properties

Now the half that runs inside a chat turn. The module docstring names three properties it owns, and they are worth quoting because each one is a constraint, not a feature.

Dormant when unconfigured. A blank URL or a blank token switches the whole path off, and there is no separate enable flag. The predicate lives here and nowhere else — app/config.py deliberately does not define it — so there is exactly one answer to "is feedback capture live in this process?":

def is_vocky_feedback_configured(settings=None) -> bool:
    return resolve_vocky_mcp_config(settings) is not None

Explainer #5 found the consequence at the other end: open_chat_agent binds record_user_feedback only if that predicate is true, and the system prompt's feedback section is gated on the same call. An unconfigured environment never advertises a tool that could only fail.

Never raises. This one is a direct consequence of explainer #5's middleware: ChatAgentMiddleware.awrap_tool_call converts any tool exception into a ChatAgentToolError, which fails the whole turn. So every failure family — DNS, TLS, timeout, 401, a 503 capture queue unavailable, an isError tool result, an unreadable receipt, a plain programming error — is swallowed into a failed CaptureOutcome and logged:

CaptureStatus         = Literal["recorded", "dormant", "failed"]
CaptureFailureReason  = Literal["blank_message", "timeout", "rejected",
                                "transport_error", "malformed_receipt"]

Two things deliberately still propagate: a genuine external cancellation (the user's turn being torn down — swallowing it would leave a zombie task awaiting), and non-Exception BaseExceptions like KeyboardInterrupt. Spotting the cancellation is subtler than it looks, because the Streamable-HTTP transport is built on anyio task groups:

def _contains_cancelled(exc: BaseException) -> bool:
    if isinstance(exc, asyncio.CancelledError):   return True
    if isinstance(exc, BaseExceptionGroup):
        return any(_contains_cancelled(sub) for sub in exc.exceptions)
    return False

Bounded. One asyncio.timeout wraps the whole attempt — connect plus initialize plus tools/call — rather than one read, and the transport's own read timeout is set to the same value as a second line of defence. The post-mortem branch reads the timeout object rather than the exception type, which is the correct way to ask "did my deadline fire?":

timeout = asyncio.timeout(config.timeout_seconds)
try:
    async with timeout:
        result = await _call_capture_tool(config, arguments)
except BaseException as exc:
    if timeout.expired():                      return _failed("timeout")
    if _contains_cancelled(exc) or not isinstance(exc, Exception):  raise
    return _fail_with_exception("transport_error", exc, token)

The call shape, and the round trip that is not made. The capture goes through client.session("vocky") + session.call_tool("capture_feedback", …) — never MultiServerMCPClient.get_tools(). get_tools() would perform a real initialize + tools/list discovery exchange, i.e. a second network hop on the turn path, to learn the name and arguments of a tool the code already knows from the contract. The process-level client cache is honest about what it buys, too: the docstring notes that client.session() opens a fresh connection and re-runs initialize every time, so the cache saves object construction rather than a socket — and a construction failure is deliberately not cached, so one bad moment cannot poison the path.

The trailing-slash trap, and why a silent failure is the worst kind. POST …/mcp without the slash answers 307 with an http:// Location — a scheme downgrade. Every conforming client drops the Authorization header across that origin change, the handshake dies on 401, and because this path is fail-open by design, the feature is silently dead: users are served perfectly and Vocky simply stays empty. The fix is defence in depth rather than an operator obligation — resolve_vocky_mcp_config appends the slash to the endpoint path (split and rejoin, so a query string is untouched) before the client is built, and both env examples ship the canonical URL with a comment naming the trap.

The module is import-clean too — no LangChain, no agent state — so we can run the real resolver against a stub settings object and watch the rules fire:

Every one of those lines is the shipped code executing; none of them touched the network. Read the _build_arguments result closely: the blank comment is dropped rather than sent as "", the all-blank tag list is omitted entirely rather than sent as [], session_id=None disappears, and user_id=42 is stringified. recorded_by is missing on purpose — on this endpoint an omitted marker is stored as "agent", which is exactly right, and sending it explicitly would only add a value that could be rejected. And _scrub exists so that the one string that must never reach a log line cannot: the bearer is replaced before the detail is truncated to 200 characters.

3e. Agent: chat/vocky_feedback_tool.py (183 lines) — the tool the model sees

Explainer #5 listed this tool as the last entry in the chat agent's registration order and deferred it to this page. Here it is. The model sees two arguments — the user's words verbatim, and the agent's own note about them — and everything else (product, integration, correlation ids) is built from the runtime context. Binding Vocky's raw capture_feedback tool, with its ~22 arguments and English docstring, was considered and rejected.

Three contracts, and the third is the one people find surprising.

Never fails a turn. The transport already never raises; the facade adds a catch-all for everything that could go wrong inside it (a hostile runtime object, a bad id coercion, a plain bug). except Exception is used precisely because it does not catch CancelledError.

Zero retrieval-budget impact. Explainer #5 showed that the four-call retrieval budget is enforced by each retrieval tool reading and incrementing retrieval_call_count in the graph state. This tool never writes that channel — and the code says so where someone might be tempted to add it:

def _ack_command(tool_call_id: str, *, attempted: bool) -> Command:
    update = {"messages": [ToolMessage(content=CHAT_AGENT_FEEDBACK_TOOL_ACK, tool_call_id=tool_call_id)]}
    if attempted:
        update[CHAT_AGENT_FEEDBACK_ATTEMPTED_STATE_KEY] = True
    # NOTE: no ``retrieval_call_count`` key, ever. That omission is the entire
    # budget-exemption mechanism.
    return Command(update=update)

One acknowledgment for every outcome. recorded, failed and dormant all return the same short, neutral Korean string:

CHAT_AGENT_FEEDBACK_TOOL_ACK = (
    "확인했습니다. 같은 내용으로 다시 호출하지 말고, "
    "이 도구에 대한 언급 없이 사용자에게 하려던 답변을 그대로 이어가세요."
)

It claims nothing ("확인했습니다" — acknowledged, not "기록했습니다" — recorded), tells the model not to call again with the same content, and tells it to carry on without mentioning the tool. The reasoning in the docstring: if the model could tell a failed capture from a successful one, it would eventually apologise, retry, or otherwise let the user learn that an internal integration is down — and a failed capture is invisible to the user by design. The distinction is kept where it belongs, in the application log.

One flag does record the difference, and its subtlety is instructive:

attempted = not outcome.is_dormant      # on ATTEMPT, not on success

feedback_capture_attempted is what the ungrounded-memory gate (explainers #5 and #11) reads to exempt a feedback turn from being rewritten — a turn spent recording a complaint naturally retrieves no documents. Marking only successes would mean a Vocky outage silently re-arms that gate on exactly the turns the feature exists for. A dormant outcome writes nothing, because nothing was attempted.

A last detail with a long shadow: the tool takes a bare ToolRuntime, never ToolRuntime[ChatAgentContext], and every context field is read through a defensive helper. A concrete generic parameter would make LangGraph build a pydantic schema for that exact dataclass and coerce the runtime context into it, which would make binding the same tool to the 컨설팅 agent (whose context type has different fields) impossible later.

What each side actually looks like from outside

  • The user, REST path: presses 👍/👎 on an answer or submits the 프로필 피드백 form, and the browser gets Vocky's receipt with 202 Accepted — or a 502/503 if the proxy could not deliver it. Feedback here is an explicit act with visible confirmation.
  • The user, agent path: sees nothing at all. They complained in conversation; the model recorded it and carried on answering. A successful capture does not even produce a container log line — the agent installs no root log handler, so its logger.info success message is suppressed and Vocky itself is the only success signal. A failure logs exactly one WARNING.
  • The operator: opens /operator/feedback, which reads Vocky, not a local table — both paths' records land in the same place — and can append a 조치 기록 (an internal handling note, never shown to the end user) or delete a record upstream, irreversibly.

Quiz

Five questions on the three leaves. Click an answer to see whether it is right and why.

1. Questions 33–40 of the 창업가 MBTI measure 준비도 (readiness). What is their relationship to the 16 types?

Only the 32 kind="personality" items feed the typology (if item.kind != "personality": continue). 준비도 is a plain additive score with bands at 33 / 25 / 17, kept in the nullable readiness_score column — deliberately not folded into axis_scores, which the views iterate per axis.

2. A user answers 보통이다 (the neutral 3) to all 40 statements. What comes back, and why?

Measured by running the shipped scorer: all-3 → GEBI with raw=24, n=8 on every axis. Because the neutral point is an integer and each item's two poles sum to 6, the exact midpoint is reachable — so the code compares doubled integers (2*raw vs 6*n) to make equality exact and gives the tie a fixed, documented winner. Determinism is what lets a stored submission be re-scored to the stored result.

3. ConsultationRequest copies the report text into content_snapshot and links its session with on_delete=SET_NULL. What is that combination buying?

The model docstring says it outright. A 30-day beat job hard-deletes anonymous guest 진단 sessions, so a consultation that only pointed at one would be empty on day 31. The same self-containment is what later licenses a hard DELETE of a request from the operator console: nothing points at the row and it points at nothing that matters.

4. The agent-side Vocky client turns every failure — timeout, 401, an isError result, even a programming error — into a failed outcome instead of raising. Why is swallowing correct here?

The docstring names the middleware as the reason. Two things still propagate: a genuine CancelledError (the user's turn being torn down — swallowing it would leave a zombie task), found by unwrapping BaseExceptionGroups from the anyio-based transport, and non-Exception BaseExceptions. There is no retry machinery at all: a capture is rare and already fail-open.

5. Vocky is reached with two different vk_ tokens. What is the split?

Two integrations, two transports, two credentials, two service scopes — verified when the feature shipped with a real docker compose config render. They also take differently-shaped URLs: VOCKY_BASE_URL is a base origin the client appends /api/feedback to, while VOCKY_MCP_URL is the full endpoint (and the agent normalises its trailing slash, because the slash-less form answers 307 with an http:// Location and the feature dies silently).
