The Fish Ladder: Catching Upbit-Local Price Spikes with Deep Limit Orders

How the arb_upbit_1 workspace went from "rest some orders 5 % away and wait for a whale" to a ₩6.4 M live book on five thin Korean markets, hedged with perpetual shorts on Bybit, armed by a two-step switch and unwound short-first. Written for a novice programmer who has never placed a limit order. Snapshot: 2026-09-19, the day the book went live for the second time.

Background

This repository started life as a market-making project on Upbit, Korea's largest crypto exchange. Its first strategy quoted a tight bid and ask around a "fair" price on the USDT-XRP pair (later USDT-ETH too), earning maker rebates during a fee promotion. That stack is history: on 2026-09-06 both maker bots and their LLM manager were stopped and liquidated to KRW (phase P15, decision D17). What replaced them is the subject of this document, and it is a very different animal. It never quotes near the price. It rests orders far from it and waits.

A beginner's primer on the words you need (skip if you know order books)
Order bookThe exchange's list of resting buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks), each at a price and size. The best bid and best ask are the touch; halfway between them is the mid. Maker / takerA maker rests an order and waits for someone to hit it. A taker crosses the spread and hits a resting order immediately. On Upbit's KRW markets both pay 0.05 %, no maker discount. Post-onlyA limit order that must rest; if it would execute immediately the exchange rejects it instead. It guarantees you are the maker. IOC"Immediate-or-cancel": a limit order that fills whatever it can right now and cancels the rest. The strategy uses it only to exit. TickThe smallest price step allowed. On Upbit KRW markets it changes by price band: 5 won between ₩5,000 and ₩10,000, 10 won from ₩10,000 up. This detail caused a real bug. Market-order sweepA big market buy consumes the asks level by level, walking the price up until it is filled. On a thin book, a modest order walks a long way. Perp shortA perpetual futures position that profits when the coin falls. Holding coin on Upbit and an equal short on Bybit makes you delta-neutral: the coin's price no longer matters to your P&L, only the difference between the two venues does. FundingA small payment perp longs and shorts exchange every 8 hours. A short can earn it or pay it; over a month it is a real line item. Kimchi premiumThe gap between a coin's KRW price on Korean exchanges and its global USDT price. It widens and converges; that convergence turns out to be where the money is.

The nets are resting limit orders; the fish are the sweeps. The word stuck, and the whole package is called arb.fish.

What counts as a fish

P11 measured spikes against a reference price fixed at the top of each hour and found lots of them. That was wrong: a bot re-quotes off the current price every couple of seconds, so a slow 30-minute drift never reaches a rung that keeps moving with it. Phase P12 replaced the definition with the one still in use:

Definition: a fish

A one-minute bar in which Upbit's price range exceeds the same coin's range on a major global exchange (Bybit or Binance) by at least g percent. In the census's own words: "trades printed on Upbit at prices the global market never reached, so an order resting at ±g from the current price would have been hit, and the move was Upbit-local." An up-fish is an ask fill; a down-fish is a bid fill. A spike that Bybit made too is not a fish.

The 60-day census over 6.58 million traded Upbit minutes confirmed the thesis in the sketch's last line. Small, thin markets had fish; the majors had almost none. KRW-BTC produced zero fish on four touches in 60 days, KRW-ETH one on twenty, while KRW-BONK produced 56.65 a day at g = 1 %. Thin books are where a sweep can walk far enough to reach a deep order.

Read the numbers honestly

Every P&L figure in this document is a model number derived from a 60-day census and a fill model. The live book has so far bought its seeds and rested its rungs; it has not yet caught a single fish. The one live measurement that exists is the hedge working as designed (see Intuition). The ₩8,000 per ₩1 M per month bar is the workspace's own threshold, set in P11, for "worth running at all".

Intuition

Take one of the five live markets, KRW-INJ, at the mid it actually traded on 2026-09-19: ₩9,530. The ladder rests three rungs on each side, at 5, 7.5 and 10 % from the mid, each worth ₩200,000. Asks are rounded away from the touch (ceiling), bids likewise (floor), and each on the tick of its own price band.

What happens after the fill

This is where the two sides of the ladder stop being mirror images. P14 measured what a fish price does afterwards, by direction:

research/p14/strategy.md, "the asymmetry"

An up-fish keeps giving: the price we sold at looks better and better for four hours, because a Upbit-local spike upward is usually a premium that then converges back toward the global price. A down-fish goes the other way: the coin we bought keeps falling. So the exit is a direction-split time stop. An ask fill is held for 14,400 seconds (four hours) and then bought back with an IOC limit that crosses the touch; a bid fill is sold back after 60 seconds. Both exits cost roughly the half-spread, measured at 0.271 % up and 0.273 % down. At g = 5 that is 2.9 % of the up leg's capture and 143 % of the down leg's, which is why the bid side is closed almost immediately: it exists to catch the occasional sharp dip, not to hold anything.

Why there is a seed and a short

To rest an ask you must own the coin. So each both-sided market first buys a seed: ₩800,000 of the coin, bought post-only at the best bid (never with a market order: "a market buy on these books would make us someone's fish"). The seed is inventory, not a position; the ask ladder is dark until it fills.

Owning ₩800,000 of a thin altcoin for weeks is itself a bet, and P13 measured the median market drifting −9.6 % a month. The hedge removes that bet: a Bybit perpetual short of the same coin quantity. Now the seed's spot value and the short's value move together and cancel; what is left is exactly the thing the fish is made of, the Upbit-versus-global gap.

Key idea: the edge is a premium, not a wick

P11 expected Upbit spikes to be local wicks that snap back. Its own data said otherwise: the Bybit perp shared a median 0.891 of every 5 % Upbit move. What the hedged ask fill actually captures is the remaining slice, a kimchi premium of about 0.5 % at the touch, roughly half of which converges within the hour. Three independent calculations in P11 reconcile to the same median hedged gain of +0.398 % per fill. That is why the ask leg is held for hours and the position is neutral to the coin's direction.

Why so few markets, and why so little capital

The catch is concentrated and lumpy. In the 60-day window, 471 fish at g = 5 % were spread across 246 rankable KRW markets, but eight markets held 65 % of them and two (BONK and STORJ) held 43 %. One KST day held 23.8 % of all fish; on the eight picked markets, 60 % of days produced no up-fish at all. Beyond rank 90 a market has no measured fish at g ≥ 5, so adding capital adds nothing. The ₩10 M deposit therefore funds a ₩6.4 M book and leaves ₩3.6 M idle on purpose.

The book also shrank by attrition. Of P14's eight picked markets, two were already flagged for delisting before P14 picked them; BONK terminated on 2026-09-07, STORJ on 2026-09-14 and ICX was declared on 2026-09-18 and left the same day. What remains: KRW-ZIL, KRW-CRV and KRW-INJ both-sided and hedged, KRW-SKR and KRW-MANTRA bid-only (SKR's short would cost ~₩120 k/month in funding against ~4 % of the catch; MANTRA has no Bybit perp at all). Five markets carry 26.78 % of P14's basket value, so the model row written into the live params is ₩18,000 per ₩1 M per month: 2.2× the bar, down from the 9–10× P14 modelled on eight.

2. The ladder: where a rung goes

ladder.py is the strategy in its purest form: a clock-free function that compares what is resting with what should be, and emits actions. The base price is one line:

def rung_price(side: str, anchor: float, rung_pct: float) -> float:
    """``anchor × (1 ∓ g)`` — a bid below the anchor, an ask above it."""
    g = float(rung_pct) / 100.0
    return anchor * (1.0 - g) if side == BID else anchor * (1.0 + g)

src/arb/fish/ladder.py:103

P15 added a book-aware refinement, the competitor nudge. If someone else's order of at least ₩50,000 rests just beyond our rung, we place one tick nearer the touch than they do, so a sweep reaches us first. The nudge is bounded on both sides: never nearer the touch than g itself (the seed bid rests there), and never past one tick inside the next rung.

Two things to notice. The hysteresis compares the current mid with the anchor the resting order was priced from, not with the last mid, so a slow drift accumulates until it crosses the threshold. And the 84-second floor applies only to the book reason, because book-driven re-quotes are what the rolling-minute budget counts. With hysteresis_frac 0.05 the tabulated rate was ~20 re-quotes a minute across the book, 17 % of Upbit's 2-per-second order pocket.

3. Sizing and the tick-band bug

One arithmetic sizes every rung and explains every refusal, so a refusal can never be explained by a second, drifting copy of the rule:

    ...

Edge case that bit: a price's tick is the tick of the price, not of the mid

Until fix P15.F4, a rung was rounded on the tick measured at the mid. On 2026-09-19 KRW-INJ traded at ₩9,530 (tick 5) while its ask rungs at +5/7.5/10 % were all above ₩10,000 (tick 10). Every ask was rounded to a multiple of 5, Upbit refused every one with invalid_price_ask, and because nothing memoised a refusal that was not post-only, the ladder re-sent it about twice a second: 17,696 refusals in nine hours. Two fixes landed the same day: round_price_banded rounds on the band of the price itself, and any refusal the engine cannot interpret now spends a per-side budget of 5 before pausing that side with one probe every 300 seconds.

5. The gate: the one strategic refusal

Before a side is quoted, the gate asks whether Upbit's recent move was shared with the major exchange. If Upbit rose 0.3 % in the last minute and Bybit rose at least a quarter of that, the move is global, the ask side would be selling into a real rally rather than a local premium, and the side is refused. P13 found this signal worth +₩25,618 per ₩1 M per month as a pre-fill gate and −₩102,958 as an exit rule, which is why it lives here and not in the closer.

Before an order is even queued, three guards can silently refuse it: the order-rate pocket being down to its last slot this second, the side resting under a place-error pause, and a memo that says this exact price at this exact touch was refused already. The first live run's 8,654 orders were all post-only and none ever crossed.

8. The hedge, in prose

hedge.py is a thousand lines of Bybit v5 API handling, so here is its policy rather than its code. The seed is hedged the instant its fill arrives (the short is sized from the fill, not from an account snapshot, after a dry run measured one short arriving 185 seconds late because the balance frame lagged the fill frame). Rung fills are not hedged one by one: a taker round trip on Bybit costs 0.11 % of notional, and a four-hour ask hold makes those fills transient, so the leg rebalances every 300 seconds only when holding and short disagree by more than 35 % of a seed. The short is always sized as contracts = coin_qty / multiplier, never from a KRW notional and an exchange rate: the notional form asked for 43 % more coin than the book held, which would have been a naked short of the difference.

9. The switch: DISARMED start, eight checks to arm, short-first OFF

A live start places nothing. The runner halts every market with reason off, pauses the seed buyer and waits for the operator:

POST /on runs eight feasibility checks. Every check runs even after one fails, so the operator sees the whole picture in one call, and a single FAIL means no arm. There is no override flag.

src/arb/fish/control.py, Controller.on

OFF is the mirror, and its order is the safety. The seed buyer is paused book-wide first (otherwise it would re-place a seed bid on the next step), then each market in sequence: halt, cancel every rung and the seed order, cross out open positions, close the short, and only if that succeeded, sell the seed. A market whose short could not be closed is reported pair_kept with its coin still held, because a sold seed beside an open short is a naked short.

10. Risk: what stops the book

The caps are judged on realised P&L only, per KST day, and the open mark is never counted. The reasoning is in the docstring: "a four-hour ask hold makes an open mark a price path rather than a loss". The function is pure so the risk stop can be tested without a loop.

    over = tuple(sorted(m for m, v in pnl_by_market.items()
                        if m not in skip and float(v) <= -abs(p.loss_cap(m))))
    total = float(sum(float(v) for v in pnl_by_market.values()))
    cap = float(p.book_daily_realised_loss_cap_krw if book_cap is None else book_cap)
    return over, total, total <= -abs(cap)

11. How the operator runs it

There is no page and no browser. The book is a host process on the operator's Mac, started in two steps from the repo checkout, and each command is run by the operator, never by an agent:

Live mode is gated three times before a socket opens: the launcher refuses without FISH_LIVE=1 (exit 2), the runner refuses without it or without credentials (exit 3), and the whole preflight runs in-process (exit 1 on any FAIL). research/p15/go_live.sh is the one-command form and deliberately exits leaving the book running. The control endpoint is 127.0.0.1:8796, loopback-only and unauthenticated; the bind address is the whole of its access control, so a second runner refuses with exit 3.

The kill switch on a hedged book is off then stop, never kill

bin/fish.sh kill predates the hedge. It cancels and crosses out on Upbit and leaves the Bybit short open. off closes each short first and confirms it before selling the seed; stop then ends the process. The one screen no agent can read is the Bybit positions page, behind the operator's own login.

12. Persistence and tests

Everything lands in a SQLite ledger at $FISH_DIR/ledger.sqlite: runs, orders (with the why of every placement), fills, positions, exits, a per-market heartbeat every 10 seconds, events and a daily table. A restart re-reads today's realised P&L across runs, so a restart is not a way to clear the day's cap, and carries open positions with their original open time.

The workspace's rule is to test only what the product cannot afford to get wrong, and tests/test_fish.py holds about fifty such cases. The ones that pin this document's claims:

  • test_the_requote_rule_holds_below_hysteresis_and_replaces_above_it_or_on_age

  • test_a_rung_is_rounded_on_its_own_tick_band_and_never_flaps_across_one

Quiz

Five questions. Click an answer for immediate feedback.

1. KRW-ZIL jumps 6 % in one minute, and in the same minute ZILUSDT on Bybit also rises 5.5 %. Under the P12 definition, is that minute a fish at g = 5 %?

A fish is a minute where Upbit's range exceeds the major's by at least g. A spike the major also made is a shared move; the gate would refuse the side for exactly this reason.

2. Why does the live book hold an ask fill for four hours but sell a bid fill back after 60 seconds?

The exit is a direction-split time stop chosen from the measured after-fill paths: an Upbit-local spike upward is usually a premium that converges, a dip usually keeps going.

3. The Bybit short is sized as contracts = coin_qty / multiplier rather than from the seed's KRW value and an exchange rate. What went wrong with the KRW-notional approach?

Decision D33: the hedge exists to neutralise the coin the book holds, so it must be sized from that coin quantity. A KRW notional divided by a display rate is a different number.

4. All three hedged markets are showing an open mark of −₩250,000 each, but no exit has been booked today. What does the ₩600,000 book cap do?

P15 replaced the mark-inclusive cap with a realised-only one (D31/D41). The test suite's first assertion for the caps is exactly that an underwater open mark trips nothing.

5. The operator wants to shut the live book down for the night. Why is bin/fish.sh off followed by stop the right sequence, and bin/fish.sh kill the wrong one?

Selling the seed while its short is still open turns a delta-neutral pair into a naked short. OFF's order (cancel, close short, then sell seed) exists to prevent exactly that; it also unwinds markets one at a time because Bybit rate-limits cancel-all at 1 req/s.
