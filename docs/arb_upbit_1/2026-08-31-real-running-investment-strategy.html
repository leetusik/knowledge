Background — the event, the capital, and the rule that decides eligibility

The event (unchanged, and still the reason any of this exists)

Upbit's 메이커 플러스+ promotion (notice 1793218405) runs 2026-08-29 00:00 → 2026-09-11 23:59 KST across the BTC and USDT markets. For API orders it pays a 0.00 % maker fee and a +0.04 % maker reward on filled notional, paid in the quote currency. A 22 % withholding applies to the whole gross reward once it passes ₩50,000 — a cliff, not a rate — so the net reward above the cliff is 3.12 bps. Taker fees are 0.05 % everywhere, and the KRW market is not in the event.

The hedge-cost fact from v2 has not moved and still shapes the design: hedging one fill on the deep KRW book costs about 8.81 bps (5.00 fee + 2.81 half-spread + 1.00 slippage) against a reward worth at most 4 bps. So profit has to come from maker-to-maker round trips inside the event market, never from crossing a spread. Both bots run band_mode: "soft" and have never hedged.

The capital, and how it got cut in half twice

Version 2 said the capital was ₩2,562,811 "because that is what was on the account". On 2026-09-02 the operator confirmed that the full ₩10,000,000 is available but not yet deposited, and then — at the open P9 acceptance gate — decided not to wait for it: "for now, we doing it with only what we got." So the ₩10M top-up (RUNBOOK §10.2) is deferred, not cancelled, and what actually ran is the two-market split at the current size:

Why the legs were hand-placed and not arb.live.convert. convert --to-layout reads the whole account and plans against one params file. With two bots sharing the USDT sleeve it would happily place an ask selling the other bot's USDT down to one file's layout. RUNBOOK §10.6 says it plainly: "There is no two-market form of convert; do not invent one." The re-cut is two hand-placed legs, sell first.

Each sleeve is laid out USDT 75 % / base 25 %, and the layout is fundable by construction exactly as before: usdt_share 0.75 ≥ target + band + size_pct/100 = 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.20 = 0.70. A full 20 % bid (≈ ₩253,600 ≈ 184 USDT) can always be paid for while the base coin sits at or below its cap. At this size the projected gross reward stays far under the ₩50,000 cliff; the cliff would only bind at the ₩5M + ₩5M split the runbook was written for.

Eligibility: the flag that stops and the flag that warns

Upbit publishes two kinds of market flag in GET /v1/market/all?isDetails=true: a boolean warning (거래유의, the investment-warning designation that removes a pair from the event — BTC-SNX carried it and was dropped in P1) and a caution dictionary (주의, automatic surge notices such as TRADING_VOLUME_SOARING or PRICE_FLUCTUATIONS). Until re-cut day either one made a market ineligible: the hourly check emitted a stop-level eligibility_failed and the bot exited 2. At 14:35 KST on 2026-09-02 Upbit set caution.TRADING_VOLUME_SOARING on USDT-XRP, preflight FAILed on it, and the XRP bot could not restart. The operator's ruling — "ignore 주의 respect 유의" — became a six-line change: only warning (plus a non-zero maker fee or a non-active market) fails eligibility; a caution flag is recorded, emits a warn-level market_caution event on every hourly check, and turns the preflight row WARN instead of FAIL. The XRP bot started under exactly that flag an hour later.

Intuition — two anchors, one sleeve, the 30-second mark, and honest fills

1. The anchor, now for two pairs

Nothing about fair value changed. Each event pair is thin and its own mid is noisy, so fair is imported from the deep KRW books behind it and then corrected for the persistent basis between that import and the pair's own mid, with a 1,800 s half-life EWMA:

f_XRP = last(KRW-XRP) / last(KRW-USDT)        f′ = f · (1 + b),  b = EWMA(own_mid / f − 1, hl 1800 s)
f_ETH = last(KRW-ETH) / last(KRW-USDT)        same formula, same half-life, its own EWMA

The quote arithmetic is the same pure function for both markets — desired_quotes() in src/arb/live/quote.py: q = base holdings in KRW ÷ capital, dev = q − target, lean = 2δ · dev, bid and ask at f′ · (1 ∓ δ − lean), clipped post-only, then the post-fill band decides whether the side may be placed at all. What differs between the two markets is six parameters, and one of them differs by a factor of ten.

2. Why δ is 14 bps on XRP and 1.4 bps on ETH

δ is how far outside f′ the quotes sit, and it has to be read against the book it sits in. The two books could hardly be more different:

On XRP the stand-off is there to avoid being picked off by anchor noise (σ ≈ 4 bps against a 14-bps δ), and the S3 re-sweep confirmed there is nothing to gain from moving it: δ 8 was the only same-capital point that beat the baseline at all (+₩211/day), and it flipped to −₩3,871/day under the legacy fill rule. A gain that changes sign across fill rules is a tie, and a tie keeps the live set. On ETH the edge is a different animal: the book is deep and tight, the reward is 79 % of the simulated alpha, and 1.4 bps is simply the innermost stand-off that still survived every mark and every fill rule in S4. Quoting 14 bps out on that book would be 340 ticks from fair and would never fill.

3. The band is unchanged; the sleeve is new

The post-fill band from v2 still governs both bots: before placing, the engine asks whether a full fill would leave inventory inside target ± band, so band is the order-size ceiling and the two-sided zone is q ∈ [0.20, 0.30]. What P9 added is the answer to a question a single bot never had to ask: whose USDT is this?

Upbit's account balance (GET /v1/accounts) and the private myAsset stream are account-wide — the stream cannot even be filtered by market. Only the base coin is per-market. So two bots on one key would each read the same 1,373 USDT and each size its bids against all of it. The fix is a clamp, not a sub-account: each bot caps the USDT it believes it holds at its own sleeve, usdt_share × capital_krw / px(KRW-USDT) = 0.75 × 1,267,965 / 1,377 ≈ 691 USDT.

The clamp is read in exactly one place downstream — the bid-side balance cap in quote._round_side — so it is literally a "do not spend the other bot's USDT" rule and nothing else. It does not reserve anything. A full-size bid is only 20 % of capital (≈ 184 USDT) per bot, so the two bots together could bid about 368 USDT against a 1,373 USDT balance, and the 9-USDT overlap between the ceilings is academic. If it ever stops being academic, the symptom is insufficient_funds rejects on one bot, and RUNBOOK §10.6 says repeated ones mean the sleeves no longer fit the account.

The same "one account" problem exists for the order rate. Upbit meters order creation per account, not per process. Two bots that each assumed the whole pocket would race each other into 429s and, eventually, an IP ban. So each bot's REST client is constructed with its own slice: max_order_req_per_s 3 in both files, 3 + 3 against the workspace's 6/s policy cap. The split was decided on data, not symmetry for its own sake: the XRP bot's live placement histogram is p50 1 / p90 2 / p99 3 / max 6 per second, with only 101 seconds at ≥ 4 in 57 hours (0.049 % of wall clock), while the ETH smoke burst to 4/s nine times in eight minutes. At 3/s each, XRP queues a placement in about 0.05 % of seconds and ETH queues one order per four-burst — a sub-second delay either way, never a dropped order.

4. Why fills are marked 30 seconds late

This is the one finding in P9 that changes the number you read every day, so it deserves the toy data. On 2026-09-01 the live report said the day's core alpha was −₩9,259 on 34 fills. P9.S2 re-marked the same 34 fills against the recorded book at several horizons:

The mechanism: our ask rests at the top of the book; a buyer sweeps through it and two more levels; the next book the bot sees has its best ask three levels higher, and the at-fill mark books that whole move as our loss on the fill that started it. Thirty seconds later the book has re-formed and the same fill marks positive. The market-microstructure name for this is a markout: the price change over a chosen interval after a fill, the standard instrument for measuring adverse selection. The old headline was a zero-second markout, and a zero-second markout on a thin book measures the sweep, not the fill.

P9 fixed it on both sides of the ledger. On the read side, the report and both dashboards now headline core alpha @30 s, recomputed per fill from the mid_30s the bot was already storing, with the at-fill number kept beside it and marked n/N on every surface (a fill younger than 30 s falls back to its at-fill value and is counted, never silently zeroed). On the write side, the sink's _mark now refuses a book mid more than 50 bps from f′ — the tape's p99.9 — and marks at f′ instead, emitting a mark_band event. That second fix matters more than it looks, because the at-fill mark is also what feeds the daily-loss stop: three post-trade-touch marks could have tripped a ₩40,000 stop on a day that was actually positive.

Two things the band is not. It is not a clamp (it falls back to f′, it does not pull the mid toward it), and it is not a freshness gate — the plan for P9.S2 expected staleness to be the bug and the tape refuted that. "Healthy" is roughly zero mark_band events per hour; 50 bps caught three fills in 3.6 days.

5. Honest fills: the R0 → R3 ladder

v2 said plainly that the backtest's full-fill rule was optimistic by roughly 2×. P9.S2 replaced the guess with a measurement. It rebuilt the real order timeline from the ledger — 15,892 orders, 15,549 acked, 59 deduplicated fills — and replayed it against the recorded tape (with our own resting orders subtracted, the "de-self" transform) under four candidate fill rules:

R2 wins on F1, never misses a real fill (recall 1.000), and sizes each fill within 0.3 % of reality. The old rule's 2× optimism turned out to live in which orders fill, not in how much of each fills — its size ratio on orders that did fill was already 1.04. The engine default became fill_model = "r2", the paper sink got the same two branches so a paper soak fills the way the backtest does, and legacy was kept and verified bit-for-bit against the pre-P9 code so every P1 number still reproduces.

The gap that was reported, not tuned away. Over the live window R2 makes 37 fills where the ledger has 59 — −37 % against a ±25 % parity bar, and −53 % on 09-01 against ±40 %. R3 is the only rule inside the bar (−23.7 %) and buys it with 2.5× fill size. R2 is right on size (₩141k per fill vs ₩149k live) and short on count. Nothing was adjusted to close the gap; the orchestrator's honesty rule (decision 6) makes every P9 number an R2 floor, and the live parity result is the reason real numbers should run higher.

6. Capacity: fills are print-sized, so capital saturates — on one market

R2 changed the answer to a question v2 never asked: what happens to core alpha as the order gets bigger? Under the old rule a bigger order filled bigger; under R2 a fill is the size of the print that hit it, and the median USDT-XRP print is ₩30–62k whatever the order is. So the fraction of an order that fills collapses from 6.0 % at ₩512k per side to 1.0 % at ₩4M per side, and the 30-s core alpha per day goes ₩2,232 → ₩3,201 → ₩4,671 → ₩4,702 at ₩2.56M / ₩5M / ₩10M / ₩20M — marginal ₩398, ₩294, then ₩3 per extra ₩1M. USDT-XRP absorbs about ₩10M and not a won more. Under legacy or R3 the curve never saturates, "precisely because those rules fill whole orders; the saturation is a property of the honest rule".

USDT-ETH does not saturate inside ₩20M (₩4,364 → ₩6,313 → ₩10,257 → ₩21,253 per day), which is the quantitative reason it is a second market rather than more XRP. Along the way inv_share — v2's honesty check — was demoted from a gate to a descriptor: over a 3.6-day window the mark on a 25 % base layout dwarfs a ₩10k alpha and the live set itself scores 1.90. The inventory condition for the rest of P9 is max |q| ≤ 0.5, which every row holds under the soft band; inv_share is still printed, and priced as directional exposure when allocating.

Part 2 — how it runs now

The launcher: every path derived from the params file

bin/bot.sh was never edited (rewriting a running bash script in place kills it mid-loop — v2's rule stands). The ETH bot runs from a new, parameterised launcher whose only input is the params file; the market inside it decides every path, and the XRP market is refused so a second XRP process can never quote beside the live one:

# bin/market-bot.sh <params.json> start|stop|status|kill   (start defaults to --stage paper)
MARKET="$("$PY" -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))["market"])' "$PARAMS")"
SLUG="$(printf '%s' "${MARKET#*-}" | tr '[:upper:]' '[:lower:]')"      # USDT-ETH -> eth

BOTDIR="${MARKET_BOT_DIR:-$ROOT/data/live}"
PIDFILE="$BOTDIR/${SLUG}_bot.pid"
SUPFILE="$BOTDIR/${SLUG}_supervisor.pid"
OUTLOG="$BOTDIR/logs/${SLUG}_bot.out"
SUPLOG="$BOTDIR/logs/${SLUG}_supervisor.out"
LEDGER="$BOTDIR/${SLUG}_ledger.sqlite"

refuse_xrp() {
  if [ "$MARKET" = "USDT-XRP" ]; then
    echo "refusing: USDT-XRP is bin/bot.sh's bot (data/live/bot.pid)." >&2
    exit 2
  fi
}
ARGS=(--params "$PARAMS" --ledger "$LEDGER" --log "$OUTLOG" --pidfile "$PIDFILE")

The supervisor loop inside it is bin/bot.sh's verbatim — the same exit-code contract (0 clean, 1 crash → restart after 10 s at most 5 per rolling hour, 2 risk stop → never, 3 refused → never), the same caffeinate -i wrapper. arb.live.bot run gained one flag, --pidfile, with the old default, so bin/bot.sh, convert, the manager and the XRP dashboard are unaffected. The second dashboard is the same program pointed at the second ledger:

DASH_PIDFILE=data/live/eth_dash.pid DASH_OUTLOG=data/live/logs/eth_dash.out \
  bin/dash.sh start --port 8778 --tailscale \
    --ledger data/live/eth_ledger.sqlite --params research/live/eth_anchor.json

The two env overrides are how bin/dash.sh finds its own process, so they go on stop and status too. --params is what labels the page: the market, the currencies, the capital and the caps card all come from the file rather than being hard-coded to XRP, and the eth_ prefix (LiveParams.file_prefix) resolves the right pidfiles for the health card. One caveat carried over from a P9 fresh-eyes finding: the dashboard caches its params at start, so after editing a params file the page reports the old capital until the dashboard is restarted (deferred D46 is the footnote that clips the path).

The sleeve clamp

# src/arb/live/orders.py — LiveSink
def sleeve_ceiling(self) -> float:
    """This bot's own share of the **shared** quote balance, in quote units."""
    snap = self.snapshot
    px = float(getattr(snap, "px_krw_quote", NAN)) if snap is not None else NAN
    if not (px == px) or px <= 0.0:
        return NAN                      # no snapshot yet: the raw balance stands, as before P9
    return float(self.p.usdt_share) * float(self.p.capital_krw) / px

def _recompute_holdings(self) -> None:
    for ccy, attr in ((self.p.quote_ccy, "hold_usdt"), (self.p.base_ccy, "hold_xrp")):
        bal, lock = self.assets.get(ccy, (0.0, 0.0))
        setattr(self, attr, bal + lock)  # a resting order still owns its funds
    self.raw_hold_usdt = self.hold_usdt
    ceiling = self.sleeve_ceiling()
    if ceiling == ceiling and self.hold_usdt > ceiling:
        self.hold_usdt = ceiling

The mark band and the 30-second headline

# src/arb/live/orders.py — LiveSink._mark (the paper sink carries the identical block)
mid = fair                                            # f′ from the snapshot
if self.book is not None:
    bb, ba = self.book.best_bid, self.book.best_ask
    if bb > 0 and ba > 0:
        mid = 0.5 * (bb + ba)
band = float(self.p.mark_band_bps)                    # LiveParams default 50.0; <= 0 disables
if (band > 0 and math.isfinite(fair) and fair > 0 and math.isfinite(mid) and mid > 0):
    dev_bps = abs(mid / fair - 1.0) * 1e4
    if dev_bps > band:
        self._event("mark_band", ident, ts, {"mid": mid, "fair": fair, "bps": round(dev_bps, 2), "band_bps": band})
        mid = fair

# src/arb/live/report.py — the headline, recomputed at read time from the stored mid_30s
def trading_pnl_30s(row: sqlite3.Row) -> tuple[float, bool]:
    at_fill = _fl(row["trading_pnl_quote"])
    mid = row["mid_30s"]
    if not _ok(mid) or float(mid) <= 0:
        return at_fill, False                         # fall back AND count it: a silent zero is a worse lie
    return mark_fill(str(row["side"]), row["price"], row["size"], float(mid)), True

Both numbers come out of the same mark_fill implementation and are converted at the same fx.at(ts) instant, so the 30-s and at-fill headlines differ only in which mid they use. Rules that bite with two bots on one account.

Never run arb.live.convert while both bots exist — it plans one file's layout against the whole account and would sell the other bot's USDT. Re-cuts are hand-placed post-only legs.

Never edit bin/bot.sh or bin/market-bot.sh while it runs; both loops are read by byte offset.

Stop the manager before any deliberate XRP restart, or it will restart the bot under you; the manager does not know the ETH bot exists, so an ETH restart needs no manager step but gets no manager safety net either.

make stack-status is the XRP stack only. The ETH bot has no Make target — its status and its supervisor log are separate commands, every time.

Restart a dashboard after editing its params file; it caches the file at start.

Never preflight --probe on a live account — it places three real ≈ 0.5-USDT orders. The eight plain checks suffice, and --market USDT-ETH selects the second file.

--override text must be plain ASCII: no ₩, no em dash, "pct" not "%".

The Mac stays awake with the lid open; caffeinate -i holds off idle sleep only. Two bots now mean up to four resting orders abandoned if it sleeps — the reason P10 (host resilience) is queued.

Best practices & next steps

Where this aligns with prevailing practice

Quoting around an inventory-skewed reservation price is still the textbook design, now twice over. Hummingbot's guide to the Avellaneda & Stoikov strategy states the rule this system's lean = 2δ·(q − target) implements: "At q = 0, the reservation price equals the market mid-price; at q < 0, the trader is short, raising the reservation price to favor buy orders; at q > 0, the trader is long, lowering the reservation price to favor sell orders", with the deviation q measured against a user-specified inventory target percentage (Guide to the Avellaneda & Stoikov Strategy — hummingbot.org). Both bots run exactly that shape with target 0.25; the difference remains that their skew comes from a risk-aversion coefficient and a volatility estimate, while ours was swept.

Marking fills at a later horizon is the standard measure of maker quality. Databento's microstructure guide defines a markout as "the price change within some arbitrary time interval before or after an event, such as a fill", calculated "mid-to-mid, trade-to-mid (fill-to-mid), or trade-to-trade" and used by liquidity providers "to determine the toxicity and adverse selection from price takers" (What are markouts — databento.com). The 30-s headline is a 30-second fill-to-mid markout plus the reward; the at-fill number was a zero-second one. P9 also already reports +5 min beside it in the backtest digests, which is the markout-curve practice the guide describes.

Modelling the queue ahead of a resting order, and letting it shrink, is what serious backtesters do. hftbacktest documents the same two mechanisms R2 combines: a risk-averse queue model where "the order queue position will be advanced only if a trade happens at the price", a probabilistic one where "the decrease in quantity happens at both before and after the queue position", and a PartialFillExchange where orders "will be executed based on the quantity of the order book" (Order Fill — hftbacktest.readthedocs.io). R2 sits between the two queue models — it advances on at-price prints and on observed shrinkage of our own level — and its partial through-fill is the partial-fill exchange. The difference is that ours was picked by scoring four rules against 15,549 real orders rather than assumed.

One writer per SQLite database, all processes on one host — and two databases is the natural way to get two writers. SQLite's own WAL page: "since there is only one WAL file, there can only be one writer at a time", and "All processes using a database must be on the same host computer; WAL does not work over a network filesystem… because WAL requires all processes to share a small amount of memory" (Write-Ahead Logging — sqlite.org). Giving the ETH bot eth_ledger.sqlite instead of a second writer on the XRP ledger is the correct reading of the first sentence; keeping both on the bare Mac (decision 21) satisfies the second.

Splitting a per-account rate limit below the published number is the conservative form of what the exchange asks for. Upbit's current developer documentation puts the order group at "up to 8 requests per second" per account and the default group at 30/s, with a remaining-req header of the form group=default; min=1800; sec=29, and warns that continuing to send after a 429 "may be temporarily blocked by the system… an HTTP 418 status code and the block duration will be returned", with "progressively longer block durations" for repeat offenders (Rate Limits — global-docs.upbit.com). The workspace caps itself at 6/s and splits that 3 + 3, a margin below the published 8; a 418 would take both bots down at once, which is why the manager treats it as the one exit-2 reason it may ask triage about.

Where this deliberately diverges — and why that is defensible here

The sleeve is a ceiling, not an allocation. A general multi-strategy framework would hold a per-strategy budget and reserve funds as orders are placed. Here each bot merely refuses to believe in more USDT than its share, and the exchange arbitrates any real collision with an insufficient_funds reject. That is legitimate because a full bid is 20 % of a sleeve (≈ 184 USDT) against a 1,373 USDT balance — the two bots cannot plausibly collide — and because the reject is already a first-class, counted, alerting event in the sink. It stops being legitimate the day the sleeves are sized close to the balance.

The conservative fill rule was kept even though it fails the parity bar. A backtester tuning for agreement with live would have moved to R3 (inside ±25 %) or blended the rules. P9 kept R2 with recall 1.000 and size ratio 1.003 and reported the −37 % count gap, because every P9 decision is a capacity decision and a floor is the right side to err on. The gap is written down in decision 13 for whoever sizes the next deposit.

Still no per-fill hedge and no automatic rebalance — v2's argument stands, and USDT-ETH strengthens it: 79 % of that market's simulated alpha is the maker reward, which any taker leg would give back several times over.

The manager watches one of the two bots. The field default would be to supervise both from one place. P9 shipped the second bot without touching the manager on purpose (a manager change is a change to the one component allowed to restart a live bot, and the phase's review was already the largest of the project). The cost is stated rather than hidden: an ETH risk stop is silent until tailed.

Five questions on the parts that changed. Click an answer to see whether it is right and why.

1. Both bots read the same account balance of 1,373 USDT and each clamps it to 0.75 × 1,267,965 / 1,377 ≈ 691 USDT. Which statement about the clamp is true?

The clamp caps hold_usdt, which only the bid-side balance cap reads; it reserves nothing at the exchange, and 2 × 691 = 1,382 already exceeds the 1,373 held. A real collision surfaces where it always did — as the exchange's reject, which the sink counts and the runbook watches.

2. Over the live window fill rule R2 produces 37 simulated fills against 59 real ones (−37 %, outside the ±25 % parity bar), while R3 lands inside the bar. Why is R2 the default anyway?

Decision 13: highest F1 wins, and the honesty rule (decision 6) says the parity gap is stated, not closed. R3's notional optimism is exactly the kind of error that made the old rule over-count by 2×.

3. On 2026-09-01 the at-fill mark gave −₩9,259 and the +30 s mark gave +₩6,909 on the same 34 fills. What was wrong with the at-fill mark?

The ledger's mid_at_fill equals the raw recorded mid at the fill instant (|Δ| p50/p90 0.0 bps), so a freshness gate would have changed nothing. The 50-bps band and the 30-s headline both address the same artefact; the realised FIFO PnL for the day was +₩2,440.

4. δ is 14 bps on USDT-XRP and 1.4 bps on USDT-ETH — a factor of ten. What justifies the difference?

One ETH tick is 0.042 bps, so δ 1.4 bps is ≈ 34 ticks and the 0.5-bps re-quote gate ≈ 12 ticks; on XRP one tick is 7.5 bps and δ 14 ≈ 1.9 ticks on a 2-tick book. The reward rate is the same 0.04 % on both markets — what differs is how much of the edge the reward is.

5. Upbit sets caution.TRADING_VOLUME_SOARING on USDT-XRP while the bot is running. After decision 25, what does the bot do?

The six-line change in risk.py / preflight.py: ok depends on warning only, a caution flag is recorded and warned about, and preflight shows WARN. The XRP bot was started under exactly that flag at 17:11 KST on re-cut day.
