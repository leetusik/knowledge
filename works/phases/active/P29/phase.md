### What each slice settles (scope, not a plan; each slice's `plan.md` is written at its turn)

**S1 — book and chapter model**
- Books: codebase books (built from a project's phases or slices) and subject books (no repo). Chapters:
  order, identity, visibility. Version history (P23) and Korean/English variants. Whether this extends
  today's `documents` or adds new entities, and where each piece lives: disk + git, the disposable SQLite
  index, or the Postgres control plane.
- A mapping of **every** existing document into books and chapters: all **42** on `origin/main`
  (`changple5` 20, `glide-ilab` 7, `knowledge` 5, `vocky` 4, `arb_upbit_1` 3, `Mijual` 1,
  `bootstrap_agentic_workspace.sh` 1, `base` 1). Includes the series (glide-ilab's p8/p9/p17-and-p18/p24,
  P26's five-part `knowledge` set, vocky's 4-part P15 set), `p8`/`p8-ko` as one chapter with two variants,
  the topic docs, and the one markdown doc (in `base`).
- How a workspace phase or slice becomes a chapter (the review's `explain: not written — run /explain for
  this phase` pointer → `/explain`): which book, position, title, and how re-explaining produces the next
  version.
- Images: attached by the agent at publish, stored where, served with the chapter's own visibility (P19:
  private by default, 404-never-403), limits and formats.
- Compatibility: `/documents/{id}` and `/@{org}/{project}/{doc-slug}` keep resolving (P25); search and
  the MCP `search` / `fetch_document` v1 contract keep working over chapters; the graph keeps working;
  everything stays free. D13 (`source_url`) is an input for general-learning citations.

**S2 — native chapter format and the legacy path**
- A survey of what the existing explainers draw (diagram kinds, widgets, figures, tables, code, callouts,
  multi-column layouts, quizzes, Korean text) with counts and examples: the evidence the block
  vocabulary is derived from. Nothing in the vocabulary is invented.
- The format: syntax (starting candidate Markdown + figure, figure row, callout, diagram, quiz); the
  diagram vocabulary (design-system styled + sanitized custom SVG); quiz types; stable block ids (resume,
  highlights, notes, quiz results); image and cross-chapter link references; KO/EN authoring; the
  sanitize-and-render model (no iframe; raw HTML never runs on the app origin).
- The legacy path: every existing HTML explainer full-screen in the reader as the **only scroller**,
  nothing measuring content height; how the reader chrome coexists with it; how native and legacy
  chapters print; a recorded fate for each of D37, D38, D39, D40, D25 (obsolete / absorbed by P30's path /
  still needed). D36 inputs (4), (7), (9) — see `## Decisions`.
- `/explain`: how the codebase modes and the general-learning mode produce native chapters (book,
  position, images, versions, variants), and what that means for the shipped skill copies and their
  parity check.
- A real blocker in the native-format direction stops the slice with `needs_operator`; it never switches
  approach silently (`intent.md`).

**S3 — reader state and review**
- Per-reader state: resume position and progress; bookmarks, highlights, notes; quiz results and the
  review schedule; reader settings (text size, theme; column width fixed). Where each is kept and for
  whom: a signed-in owner or member, or an anonymous reader of a public project.
- How anchors survive a new chapter version and a language switch.
- Spaced repetition from quizzes or highlights: the review item, the scheduling algorithm (one
  recommended, with reasons), and the data contract for the review surface.

**DECOMP2 — the design cut**
- Co-work slices (`--kind co-work --risk high`) in waves of up to about 4 jobs; one job per surface,
  foundations first; each job's critique level (`off | critic | critic+judge`); anything the findings show
  is still missing from the inventory below.

### Build inventory (provisional; DECOMP2 finalizes it)

What to design, not how; one line per candidate. Every surface at phone, iPad and desktop.
1. Library: books by subject and by project, with progress and a resume entry.
2. Book page: the ordered chapters, progress, resume, and which variants and versions exist.
3. Reader: a native chapter in continuous scroll — fixed column, text size, theme (light/dark/sepia),
   progress, previous/next and the chapter list, resume, the KO/EN switch, version history.
4. Chapter blocks: figure, figure row (2–3 items, stacking on a phone), callout, diagram vocabulary, code,
   image, quiz.
5. Highlights, notes and bookmarks: made in the reader, listed per book.
6. Quiz: in-chapter, results saved.
7. Review: the spaced-repetition queue built from quizzes and highlights.
8. Legacy reader: an existing HTML explainer full-screen inside the reader.
9. Navigation: console, auth and landing change only where the new navigation forces it.

Out: page-turn mode, offline reading, product code, and a mockup unless the operator asks for one.
Co-work slices and their jobs: none yet — DECOMP2 records them here.
