### What each job draws

Every job covers **phone, iPad and desktop, in light, dark and sepia**. Its scope comes from the S1–S3
contracts in `## Decisions` and the per-wave notes in `## Notes for later slices`.

- **`reader`** (S4):
  - **Pages:** the chapter opening (title, the `summary` lede, the generated table of contents); reading
    (the chrome hides on scroll, with a progress indicator); the chapter end (the chapter list with
    unopened / started / finished / Updated, Mark as finished or unread, the next chapter); the settings
    panel (text size in 5 steps; theme System / Light / Dark / Sepia; no column width); the language
    switch (endonyms "English" / "한국어") and version history, including the stale-translation flag.
  - **The prose kit:** headings, lists, callouts (6 types, collapsed and expanded), code (titled,
    wrapping, ASCII `text`), tables (wide, long, numeric), links and the "missing link" style.
  - **The chapter loading state** (D36 (6)).
  - **Member and anonymous chrome.** Chrome copy is English-only (D36 (4)). The theme follows System for
    every reader, the one exception to P28's light public surfaces.
- **`figures`** (S5): `figure` (column and wide, image loading and missing, caption and credit); `figures`
  rows of 2 and 3 mixed items, side by side and stacked on a phone (a row's diagram item shows the sibling
  `diagrams` job's look); the `svg` block, themed by tone classes; `sources`, with inline citations.
- **`diagrams`** (S5): the six kinds (flow with groups, lanes, the entity shape and a loop; state;
  sequence; stack, nested too; timeline; chart as bar, line and interval) with every tone; Korean labels;
  the widest case on a phone (scale or scroll is the drafter's call, flagged for the operator); the title
  and caption as the text alternative.
- **`quiz`** (S5): every state of choice (single and multi), cloze and recall; retry; the saved answer on
  return ("your answer, v3"); anonymous: answerable, with "Sign in to save".
- **`annotations`** (S5): the bookmark toggle; select → highlight; notes on a highlight and on a block;
  edit and delete; the "Review this" toggle; the chapter's annotation panel and its Unanchored group; the
  `block` margin marker and the other-language marker; the per-book list of bookmarks and notes; legacy and
  `md` chapters ("bookmark this position", "Highlights are not available"); "Sign in to save".
- **`library`** (S6): the member library by project and by subject, including the state with no subject
  book (today's truth) and, from P29.S6, a subject's loose articles beside its books (a subject with
  articles and no book); Continue reading; book cards with progress, and loose documents; the phone loading
  skeleton (D36 (6)); the public org library `/@{org}`; Library and Review as entry points in the app
  shell; "clear all reading data", behind a confirmation (P29.S6). Console, auth and landing change only
  where this forces it.
- **`book-page`** (S6): the ordered chapters with their states, book progress, and Continue reading; each
  chapter's KO/EN variants and versions; legacy and native chapters side by side; a `phases` book, a
  per-slice book, and a preface-like chapter; the member and public views; the per-book list's entry
  (`annotations` 8); "clear this book's reading data", behind a confirmation (P29.S6).
- **`review`** (S6): the queue header (due, new highlights, done today, by book); front and back for
  choice, cloze, recall and highlight cards; Again / Good with interval previews, and Next for the graded
  types; suspend; done for today; empty; the link back into the chapter.
- **`legacy-and-print`** (S6): the full-screen frame under a slim chrome bar (back, title, previous/next,
  chapter list, KO/EN, versions, Print); "theme, text size and highlights don't apply here"; loading and

### Book and chapter model — data contracts (P29.S1; evidence in `slices/P29.S1/result.md`)

- **Entities (P29.S1).** `Book 1─* Chapter 1─* Variant(lang) = one Document 1─* Version`.
  - The **document** stays today's unit: `rel_path`, slug, P23 version chain, search, MCP and graph are
    all unchanged.
  - A **chapter** is a thin entity that points at one document per language.
  - A **book** is an ordered list of chapters.
  - **Loose documents** (in no book) remain first-class. The library lists them under their project; the
    reader opens them with no book navigation. A loose document may carry a `subject` label, and the
    by-subject view lists it under that subject beside the subject's books; a one-article subject is one
    (amended, accepted at P29.S6).
- **Book (P29.S1).** Fields:
  - `slug` (`[a-z0-9-]`, unique per project);
  - `title`;
  - `kind`: `codebase` | `subject`;
  - `subject`: a display label, required for `subject`, optional for `codebase`;
  - `description?`;
  - `chapters[]`.

  Rules:
  - A book belongs to **exactly one project**, because P19 visibility is per project. Identity is
    `(tenant, project, book slug)`.
  - A project may hold 0..n books plus loose documents.
  - A **codebase** book lives in the repo's project (`project` = the repo's directory name).
  - A **subject** book lives in a project named for its subject: `project` = the subject slug, e.g.
    `finance`, `english`, `ai`. Visibility therefore toggles per subject shelf.
  - Library grouping keys: **by project** = `project` (codebase books plus loose documents); **by
    subject** = `book.subject` for a book and the document's own `subject` for a loose document (amended,
    accepted at P29.S6).
- **Chapter (P29.S1).** A manifest entry with these fields:
  - `key`: `[a-z0-9-]`, unique in its book, never reused.
  - `position`: a JSON number; ascending order, ties broken by `key`. A fractional value inserts
    without renumbering. For phase chapters, position = the phase number.
  - `title?`: an override. Absent means the shown variant's document title.
  - `variants`: `{<lang>: <rel_path>}`, where `lang` is a BCP-47 primary subtag (`en`, `ko`, …).
  - `source?`: e.g. `{"phase": "P8"}` or `{"phase": "P26", "slice": "P26.S2"}`.

  Each document is in **at most one** chapter. Chapter identity is `(tenant, project, book, key)`, which
  is durable across a reindex. The **format flag** is per document (it can differ per variant and per
  version): `html` (legacy, iframe path), `md` (plain Markdown, rendered natively today), or `native`
  (S2 defines it). It must be derivable from the file alone (extension or header), so a reindex
  re-derives it.
- **Versions × variants (P29.S1).** Each variant is its own document with its **own independent P23
  chain**; there is no joint version counter. A publish may record
  `translated_from: {lang, version}` in that variant's header, so the reader can flag a stale
  translation. For example, `p8` is EN v2 / KO v1 today.

  What the reader's switch needs per chapter: `variants[] = {lang, doc_id, rel_path, version, format,
  updated_at, translated_from?}`. Each variant's version list comes from the existing `/versions` routes.
- **Where each piece lives (P29.S1).**
  - **Book manifest, on disk:** `<root>/.books/<project>/<book>.json`. It owns the book fields plus
    chapter key, position, title, variants and source. Writes are server-only, under the write lock.
    Tenant #1 commits it together with its chapter, in the same scoped commit. Other tenants keep it
    under `tenants/<uuid>/`, uncommitted.
  - **Document:** `lang:` is added to the header. It is optional; absent means the manifest's variant
    key, else `en`.
  - **SQLite:** `books`, `chapters` and `chapter_variants` are rebuilt from the manifests by
    `server/reindex.py` (a third walk), so a DB wipe loses nothing.
  - **Postgres:** keeps only the org slug and project visibility (unchanged), plus S3's per-reader
    state.
  - Existing files are **never rewritten** to bind them into books; only manifests are written.
- **Images (P29.S1).**
  - **Storage:** `<root>/.assets/<project>/<date>-<slug>/<sha256>.<ext>`, per tenant root,
    content-addressed and immutable. Bytes are shared across versions and variants of the same document.
  - **Identity and reference:** the publish carries additive
    `assets: [{name, media_type, content_base64}]`. The server verifies magic bytes and stores each
    asset by hash. The version's header records `assets: {<name>: <sha256>.<ext>}`, and the body
    references the **name**; S2 designs the block syntax on top of that name. Each archived version
    keeps its own name map, so old versions render with their own images.
  - **Formats and limits:** `png`, `jpeg`, `webp` and `gif` only. **No SVG upload** (S2's sanitized
    inline SVG covers vector art). At most 2 MB per image, 20 per publish, and 12 MB per request.
    JPEG and WebP metadata (EXIF/GPS) are stripped server-side (a P30 dependency choice).
  - **Serving:** `GET /app/documents/{id}/assets/{sha256}.{ext}`, and the pretty
    `/@{org}/{project}/{slug}/assets/{file}`. Behind them is the same gate as `/raw`
    (`_resolve_readable_doc`: member, or a public project), and a hash not referenced by any version of
    that document is a 404. Every miss is the constant 404 (404-never-403). Responses carry the sniffed
    `Content-Type`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none';
    sandbox` and `Cache-Control: private` (unless the project is public). Raster bytes only, so nothing
    runs on the app origin.
  - **Deletion:** deleting a document removes its asset directory. Assets otherwise live as long as
    any version references them.
  - **Git (tenant #1):** see Operator Question 2. The default is "not committed".
- **Phase or slice → chapter (P29.S1).**
  - **Phase explain:** book `phases` in the repo's project (created on first use, title
    `"<project>, phase by phase"`). Key `p<N>` (a joint explain → `p<N>-p<M>`), position `N`, title
    `"P<N>: <phase name>"`, `source.phase`.
  - **One doc per slice:** book `p<N>` (title `"P<N>: <phase name>"`), key = the slice id lowercased
    (`s1`, `f1`), position = the slice's `order`, `source.slice`.
  - **General mode:** a loose document in the subject's project (`project` = the subject slug, as under
    Book), carrying its `subject` label, by default; a chapter in a subject book only when the request
    names one (amended, accepted at P29.S6).
  - **What `/explain` sends** (all additive to `POST /api/documents`): `book: {slug, title?, kind,
    subject?}`, `chapter: {key, position?, title?, source?}`, `lang`, `translated_from?`, `assets?`, and
    `subject?` for a loose document, stored with the document (amended, accepted at P29.S6).
  - **Server placement:** the server finds or creates the manifest. It resolves `(project, book, key,
    lang)`: if that variant exists, the publish is **its next version** (the same rel_path, which
    replaces the skill's list-and-match step for chapters); otherwise it creates the document and adds
    the variant. A new chapter with no `position` is appended at max+1.
  - **Without `book`/`chapter`:** today's behavior exactly (a loose document).
- **Legacy → native re-explain (P29.S1).** The first native publish onto an `html` variant is a
  **format-migration version**:
  - The html body is archived as `.versions/<project>/<date>-<slug>/vNNNN.html` (the archive directory
    is already keyed by stem).
  - The native body is written at `<date>-<slug>.<native ext>`, and the html file is removed.
  - The SQLite row keeps its `id` (its rel_path is updated in place), and the manifest variant is
    re-pointed.
  - The old rel_path stays resolvable as an **alias**: a by-path or MCP `rel_path` miss falls back to
    the stem.
  - The pretty URL is unaffected, because it is keyed by `(project, slug)`.
  - `md → native` is an **ordinary next version**: the same rel_path, no rename, no alias (amended,
    accepted at P29.DECOMP2). Only `html → native` is a format migration; every other format change stays
    a 422.
  - P30 must make `parse_archive_rel_path` and the `document_versions` keying stem-based.
- **Compatibility (P29.S1).** Every change is **additive**:
  - `/documents/{id}` and `/@{org}/{project}/{slug}`: unchanged (P25 newest-wins).
  - **The reader's URLs (new):**
    - `/library` — the member's library;
    - `/@{org}` — the org's public library; the route is unused today;
    - `/@{org}/{project}/books/{book}` — a book. These are 4 segments, so they cannot collide with
      3-segment doc slugs;
    - `/@{org}/{project}/books/{book}/{key}[?lang=&v=]` — a chapter. A legacy chapter renders its
      document through the existing raw path.
  - `POST /api/documents`: new optional fields, `book`, `chapter`, `lang`, `translated_from` and
    `assets`. The `html → native` 422 is relaxed.
  - **New routes:**
    - `GET /api/books?project=`;
    - `GET`/`PUT /api/books/{project}/{book}` — the bearer write upserts the manifest and binds existing
      documents, which is how P30 migrates the 42;
    - `/app/books…`;
    - `/app/orgs/{org}/{project}/books/{book}`, public-gated by the project.
  - **Search:** FTS and embeddings over current documents, unchanged. Hits may gain
    `book`/`chapter_key`/`lang`.
  - **MCP v1:** `search`/`fetch_document` keep their shape. Optional output fields `book`, `chapter_key`
    and `lang` are allowed by the contract, and `fetch_document`'s `format` gains the value `"native"`, a
    new value in an existing field, additive under `mcp-server/CONTRACT.md` §Versioning (amended, accepted
    at P29.DECOMP2; detail in S2 *Ingest, search and MCP*).
  - **Graph:** nodes and edges unchanged (documents, `related`).
  - **Free:** no metering and no paid dependency.
  - **No breaking change.**
- **D13 (P29.S1).** **Not absorbed. It stays deferred.** General-learning citations are many in-body
  sources: S2's Sources block, which the research mode already writes as `#sources`. D13 is one
  public-origin URL per document. `_citation_url` stays `""`. A later refinement could return a
  chapter's public pretty URL, which is not login-gated, but that is D13's trigger to decide, not P30's.

### Native chapter format and legacy path — data contracts (P29.S2; evidence in `slices/P29.S2/result.md`)

- **Syntax (P29.S2).** A native chapter is **CommonMark + GFM** (tables with alignment, strikethrough,
  autolinks) plus exactly three extensions:
  - **callouts** as GFM alerts (`> [!WARNING] Optional title`);
  - **typed fenced blocks** ```` ```<type> #<id> ```` whose body is **YAML 1.2 core schema** (JSON
    accepted; no anchors, aliases or tags). Types: `figure`, `figures`, `diagram`, `svg`, `quiz`, `sources`.
    String fields marked *(md)* are inline markdown;
  - **heading ids** as a trailing `{#id}` (`## Background {#background}`).

  **No raw HTML**: an HTML block or inline HTML is a 422 at ingest and is never rendered. An unknown fence
  type renders as a plain code block; an unknown alert type renders as `NOTE` with its title. One
  grammar, two parsers (Python markdown-it-py; TS remark under react-markdown) held together by a shared
  set of conformance fixtures (`native source → normalized block tree JSON`) that both test suites run —
  P30's core test. Rejected: directives, MyST/Markdoc, MDX, sanitized HTML, Mermaid (reasons in
  `result.md` §2).
- **Header (P29.S2).** The `---` YAML header of a native file adds `format: native` and `syntax: 1`, plus
  S1's `lang`, `assets`, `translated_from`, and an optional `summary` (the lede shown under the title).
  The reader generates the table of contents from h2/h3; authors never write one.
- **Callout (P29.S2).** `> [!TYPE] title?` followed by a markdown body (full nesting). Types: `NOTE`,
  `IMPORTANT` (the corpus's "key idea" box), `WARNING`, `CAUTION`, `SKIP` ("skip this if you know…"),
  `DEFINITION`. A `-` right after the type (`> [!DEFINITION]- Primer`) renders it **collapsed** (a
  disclosure). No id; it is addressed through its section (see Ids).
- **Figure (P29.S2).** ```` ```figure #id ````: `src` (an asset **name**, raster only), `alt` (required,
  non-empty), `caption` *(md, required)*, `credit?`, `width?: column | wide`.
- **Figure row (P29.S2).** ```` ```figures #id ````: `caption?` *(md)*, and `items` with **2–3** entries;
  each item is one of:
  - `{image: <asset name>, alt, caption?}`;
  - `{diagram: <diagram body>, caption?}`;
  - `{svg: <svg source>, label, caption?}`;
  - `{card: {title, body (md), tone?}}` — a side-by-side text card.

  The items are side by side at tablet and desktop width and **stack on a phone**.
- **Diagram (P29.S2).** ```` ```diagram #id ```` with `kind`, `title` (required: the short text
  alternative), `caption?` *(md)*. Colour comes only from a fixed set of **tones** mapped to
  design-system tokens, so light, dark, sepia and print all apply: `default`, `muted`, `accent`, `ok`,
  `warn`, `bad`, `store`, `series-1…6`. Kinds and layouts — **none needs a general graph-layout engine**:
  - `flow` — `nodes[{id, label, sub?, at?: [row, col], shape?: box | pill | store | entity, fields?[],
    tone?}]`, `groups?[{id, label, nodes[]}]` (lanes and layers), `edges[{from, to, label?, style?: solid |
    dashed, tone?}]`, `direction?: down | right`. The author places nodes on a grid (`at`); with no `at`,
    nodes flow in `direction`. Edges are routed orthogonally. Covers pipelines, loops, funnels,
    architecture maps and entity diagrams (`shape: entity` + `fields`).
  - `state` — `flow`'s grid layout plus `initial` and `final` and labelled transitions.
  - `sequence` — `actors[{id, label}]`, `steps[{from, to, label, style?, note?}]`: one column per actor and
    one row per step.
  - `stack` — `layers[{label, items?[], tone?}]`, `nested?: bool`: fixed rows.
  - `timeline` — `axis{unit, from, to, ticks?}`, `lanes[{label, bars[{from, to, label?, tone?}],
    marks?[{at, label}]}]`: fixed rows on a scaled axis.
  - `chart` — `type: bar | line | interval`, `x{label, categories? | numeric}`, `y{label, min?, max?}`,
    `series[{name, values[], tone?}]`, `rules?[{at, label}]` (threshold or mean lines).
  - Anything else (UI mocks, matrices, decision trees, bespoke art) uses `svg`.

  Limits: 60 nodes, 100 edges, 12 lanes, 200 data points. Every label may be Korean.
- **Custom SVG (P29.S2).** ```` ```svg #id ```` whose body is one `<svg>` element, plus `label`
  (required: the text alternative) and an optional `caption`. **Reject at ingest, never strip**: each
  violation gives a 422 that lists the block id, the element and the attribute.
  - **Allowed elements:** `svg g defs marker title desc rect circle ellipse line polyline polygon path
    text tspan`, and `use` only with a local `#id`.
  - **Allowed attributes:**
    - geometry and transform: `x y x1 y1 x2 y2 cx cy r rx ry width height d points viewBox transform
      preserveAspectRatio refX refY markerWidth markerHeight markerUnits orient`;
    - markers: `marker-start/mid/end="url(#local)"`;
    - presentation: `fill stroke stroke-width stroke-dasharray stroke-linecap stroke-linejoin opacity
      fill-opacity stroke-opacity font-size font-weight text-anchor dominant-baseline letter-spacing
      vector-effect`;
    - other: `id class role aria-label aria-hidden`.
  - **Forbidden:** `script`, `foreignObject`, `style` (both the element and the attribute), every `on*`
    attribute, `image`, `a`, `animate`/`set`/`animate*`, `filter`, any `href`/`xlink:href` that is not a
    local `#id`, any `url()` that is not a local `#id`, `<!DOCTYPE>`/entities, and foreign namespaces.
  - **Colour:** `fill`/`stroke` may only be `none` or `currentColor`; all other colour comes from the
    semantic classes `kb-fill-<tone>` / `kb-stroke-<tone>` (the tones above). Hex, `rgb()` and named
    colours are rejected.
  - **Limits:** `viewBox` required; 64 KB of source; 2,000 elements. Fonts are the reader's.
  - **Render:** the reader parses the stored source and **maps the allowlisted tree to React elements**,
    dropping anything off-list a second time (defense in depth). Never `innerHTML` and never
    `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`. Strokes use `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, so hairlines survive
    DPR and scaling.
- **Code (P29.S2).** A standard fence: ```` ```<lang> title="path/or/label" #id? ````. `lang` is optional
  (`text` for ASCII art). It is highlighted at **render time on the server** into token classes styled by
  design tokens (P30 picks the library, e.g. Shiki with a CSS-variables theme). Long lines wrap; there is
  no horizontal page scroll. **Not in v1:** line marks, diffs, line numbers.
- **Quiz (P29.S2).** ```` ```quiz #id ```` with `title?` and `items` (1–20), each with a required
  `id` and `type`:
  - `choice` — `prompt` *(md)*, `options[{id, text (md)}]` (2–6), `answer: [option ids]` (more than one
    means "select all that apply"), `explain` *(md)*;
  - `recall` — `prompt` *(md)*, `answer` *(md)*, `explain?`: revealed, then **self-graded** as `got` or
    `missed`;
  - `cloze` — `text` *(md)* with blanks `{{accepted|alternative}}` (1–5 blanks), `explain?`: typed
    answers compared after trimming whitespace, case-folding and NFC normalization.

  **Grading is client-side.** The key ships in the page; this is a learning tool. A single-answer
  `choice` grades on tap; a multi-answer `choice` and `cloze` grade on **Check**. After grading the reader
  shows the right answer and `explain`. Retry is allowed, but the first attempt is the one that counts for
  review.
  - **States per item:** unanswered, answered-correct, answered-wrong (right answer shown), revealed
    (`recall` before its self-grade), self-graded.
  - **Saved result** (one per item attempt):
    `{project, book?, chapter_key?, doc, unit, lang, version, block_id, item_id, item_type, response:
    {selected:[ids]} | {blanks:[strings]} | {self_grade: "got" | "missed"}, correct: bool, attempt: int,
    answered_at}`. `doc` is the document stem `<date>-<slug>`, and `unit` is S3's cross-language unit,
    added by the server (amended, accepted at P29.DECOMP2: `doc_slug` became `doc`, and `unit` was added).
    A loose document sends `doc` with no `book`. S3 owns where it is kept.
- **Sources (P29.S2).** ```` ```sources #sources ```` with
  `items[{id, title, url, publisher?, date?, accessed?, note?}]`. Inline citations are ordinary links to
  `#<source id>`. Required in `/explain`'s general mode.
- **Ids (P29.S2).** Syntax `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,63}`, unique per document. **Carried by** h2–h4,
  every typed block, every quiz item, every quiz option and every sources item.
  - **Not carried by** paragraphs, lists, tables, code and callouts. A position inside them is
    `{block_id: the nearest preceding id'd heading or block, quote: {exact, prefix, suffix}}` (a W3C
    TextQuoteSelector). That is S3's highlight and note anchor.
  - **Assignment:** the author (`/explain`) writes them. At ingest the server assigns any missing id —
    the slug of an ASCII heading, else `s-<n>`, `<type>-<n>` or `item-<n>` — **and writes it into the
    stored file**, returning the assigned ids in the response.
  - **Checks:** a malformed or duplicate id is a 422.
  - **Survival:** a re-explain reads the previous version through `fetch_document`, keeps the id of every
    section, block and item that survives, and mints new ids for new ones. A retired id is never reused.
  - **Translations** copy every id verbatim; ids are never translated. A publish with `translated_from`
    must carry the **same quiz item and option id sets** as that source version (otherwise a 422).
    Heading and block id differences come back as warnings.
- **References (P29.S2).** Every reference is a markdown link or image URL with one of these schemes:
  - `asset:<name>` — an image (figure, figure row, or `![alt](asset:name)`). It resolves through the
    version's `assets` map to S1's `/assets/{sha256}.{ext}` route behind the BFF. An unknown name is a
    422.
  - `chapter:<book>/<key>[?lang=xx][#id]` (same project) or `chapter:<project>/<book>/<key>…` — resolves
    to `/@{org}/{project}/books/{book}/{key}`. A miss at ingest is a **warning, not a 422**, because a
    series may link forward.
  - `doc:<slug>` or `doc:<project>/<slug>` — a loose document, resolving to its pretty URL. A miss is a
    warning.
  - `#<id>` — in-chapter; a miss is a 422.
  - `http(s):` — external; opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. External image URLs are a
    422. No other scheme is allowed.

  **At render**, an unresolvable or unreadable target (missing, or private to this reader) renders as
  plain text with a "missing link" style. It is never a dead link and never a leak (404-never-403).
- **Format flag and `md` (P29.S2).**
  - **Detection:** a native file is `<date>-<slug>.md` whose header says `format: native`. Reindex
    derives `format` from the header; the extension alone still means `md` or `html`.
  - **Plain `md`** renders through the **same reader pipeline** with typed fences and `{#id}` inactive
    (they show as code and text) and with no ingest validation. The one `base` md document renders
    unchanged.
  - **`md → native`** is an ordinary next version, not a format migration (written into S1's *Legacy →
    native re-explain*; amended, accepted at P29.DECOMP2).
- **Ingest, search and MCP (P29.S2).**
  - **Ingest:** `POST /api/documents` accepts `format: "native"`. The server parses the body, validates
    the header, blocks, ids, references and SVG, writes the normalized file, and returns warnings and
    assigned ids. Every failure is one 422 that lists every violation.
  - **Limits:** 512 KB per native body, 300 typed blocks, and the per-block limits above. Today there is
    **no body limit at all** for any format; P30 should add one for every format.
  - **Search:** the FTS and embedding text is a **plain-text projection**: prose, plus figure captions
    and alt text, diagram titles, labels and captions, SVG labels, quiz prompts, options and explanations
    (never the answer keys), and source titles.
  - **MCP:** `fetch_document` returns the **native source** in `markdown` (ids included, so a re-explain
    can carry them) with `format: "native"`. This is a new value in an existing field. It is additive by
    `mcp-server/CONTRACT.md` §Versioning, and since the source is valid CommonMark a consumer that does
    not know the value can treat it as `md`. Record it in CONTRACT.md (amended, accepted at
    P29.DECOMP2).
- **Render model (P29.S2).** The stored file is read by the FastAPI read route, which returns the native
  source and the version's asset map. A Next **server component** parses it (remark + the own plugin +
  `yaml`), validates it again, and maps the result to React components. Only the quiz is a client
  island; the figure row is CSS only. **No iframe, no raw HTML, no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, no
  client-side markdown parser.** Images load from the BFF asset relay (same origin, raster, `nosniff`).
  - **CSP:** the format needs no CSP relaxation. The app ships **no CSP today** (only `X-Frame-Options`);
    P30 should add one as defense in depth (`object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; frame-src 'self';
    img-src 'self'`).
  - **Korean typography:** the article carries `lang` from the variant. Under `:lang(ko)` it uses
    `word-break: keep-all; overflow-wrap: anywhere; line-break: strict` and a font stack with a Hangul
    face before the generic fallback. Korean terms inside English chapters (27/41 legacy docs) must get
    Hangul glyphs from the fallback without `keep-all`.
- **Legacy path (P29.S2).** Every `html` document or version opens in a **legacy reader**.

  - **Layout:**

  - **Sandbox:** `allow-scripts allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox`. Still **no**

### Reader state and review — data contracts (P29.S3; evidence in `slices/P29.S3/result.md`)

- **Who keeps state, and where (P29.S3).**
  - **Signed-in reader, member or not:** all durable reader state lives in **Postgres, keyed by
    `user_id`**, never by the reader's tenant. A member of the book's org and a signed-in user reading
    another org's public project get the **same** features and the same tables; only the readability
    check differs (see Isolation).
  - **Anonymous reader:** the page, plus the **reader-settings cookie**. Quizzes are answerable in the
    page and are not saved; resume, progress, bookmarks, highlights, notes and review show a **"Sign in
    to save"** prompt that returns to the same chapter and block. No device store, so **nothing merges on
    sign-in** (Operator Question 6 holds the alternative). The prompt depends on D18 (login `returnTo`)
    and D32 (the guard's return address): P30 must ship both or the prompt drops the reader at the console.
  - **Reader settings, every reader:** a per-device cookie `kb_reader`, written by the browser and read
    by the server for the first paint (the `kb_rail` pattern: `SameSite=Lax`, not `httpOnly`, one year,
    a tampered value parses as the default). **Not synced** to the account: a phone and a desktop want
    different text sizes.
  - **Never stored in** the content tree, git, the SQLite index, `localStorage` or `sessionStorage`.
- **Keys (P29.S3).**
  - **The content owner:** `tenant_id` (the book's org, FK `tenants`) + `project` (the name, as every
    content path keys it; there is no FK to `projects`, because a document's project need not have a
    row).
  - **The variant key is the document stem** `doc` = `<date>-<slug>` (the rel_path without its
    extension), plus the `unit` below — not the chapter identity `(tenant, project, book, key, lang)`
    (amended, accepted at P29.DECOMP2). It is unique per document, durable across a reindex, survives
    the `html → native` migration (S1 keys the archive and the alias by stem) and survives binding a loose
    document into a book later. **A chapter variant and a loose document are keyed the same way.** Rejected:
    `(project, slug)`, because a re-publish without `new_version` creates a second row with the same
    slug and newest-wins would move a reader's state onto another document; `documents.id`, as S1
    forbids.
  - **The unit** `unit` = `c:<book>/<key>` for a chapter, `d:<doc>` for a loose document. It groups
    what is shared across languages (chapter progress, quiz items, review cards), because S2 makes ids
    identical across variants. The server derives it from the index on every write; a manifest change
    that binds or unbinds a document re-writes `unit` on that document's rows.
  - **Every row also stores** `lang` and the `version` it was made on.
- **Resume and progress (P29.S3).**
  - **Resume** is one position per `(user, tenant, project, doc)`: `{version, anchor_id?, fraction,
    max_fraction}`. A native position is the id of the topmost visible id'd heading or block, plus
    `fraction`; a plain `md` position is `fraction` only (it has no ids); a legacy position is the
    bridge's `{anchor, fraction}`.
  - **Restore:** by `anchor_id` when it exists in the opened version, else by `fraction`.
  - **Continue reading:** per book, the most recently updated position among its chapters; overall, the
    most recent positions across every readable book and loose document. Both are queries over the
    positions, not stored pointers.
  - **Chapter state:** `unopened` (no row) → `started` (first open) → `finished`. **Finished** = the
    end of the chapter has been reached (`max_fraction >= 0.95`, or the end-of-chapter sentinel entered
    the viewport), or the reader pressed **Mark as finished**. The same rule holds for native, `md` and
    legacy chapters (the bridge reports the fraction). Quizzes are not required. **Mark as unread**
    resets the chapter for this reader: state `started`, `max_fraction` 0, `finished_at` and
    `finished_version` cleared, and the saved position cleared to the top. The row stays. Reading to the
    end again finishes the chapter again with no press. This was amended at P29.S4's sign-off, from the
    operator's answer 10 to Operator Question 9 (`jobs/01-reader/rounds/02-revise/build-prompt.md` →
    *Progress, section and finished*).
  - **Finishing any variant finishes the chapter.** A chapter whose newest version is newer than the
    version it was finished on stays finished and shows **Updated**.
  - **Book progress** = finished chapters ÷ **every** chapter in the manifest, whatever its format or
    languages.
- **Bookmarks (P29.S3).** Explicit, any number per variant, separate from resume: `{anchor: {block_id} |
  {anchor?, fraction} (legacy), label?, snippet}`, where `snippet` is the first ~140 characters of the
  block's text (or the heading). Toggled per block. A legacy or plain `md` chapter's bookmark is its
  current position.
- **Highlights and notes (P29.S3).** One entity, an **annotation**:
  - a **highlight** = an anchor with a quote, optionally carrying a **note**;
  - a **block note** = an anchor with only a `block_id` and a note.

  So a note attaches to a highlight **or** to a block. One colour in v1. Native chapters only; plain
  `md` and legacy chapters have none (`md` has no ids).
- **Quiz results (P29.S3).** S2's saved-result payload, with `doc` (the stem) and the server-added `unit`
  (amended, accepted at P29.DECOMP2), kept as an **append-only** attempt log. The server assigns
  `attempt`. The **first attempt per `(user, unit, item_id)`, in any language and on any version**,
  counts: it seeds the review card. Later attempts are stored and the reader shows the latest one on
  return ("your answer, v3"), but they never change the card.
- **Tables: one additive revision, `alembic/versions/0006_reader_state.py` (P29.S3).** Every table has
  `user_id` FK `users` **ON DELETE CASCADE** and `tenant_id` FK `tenants` **ON DELETE CASCADE**; every
  user-created row has a **client-generated UUID** `id` (idempotent retries); timestamps are
  `timestamptz`.
  - `reader_positions`: `user_id, tenant_id, project, doc, unit, lang, version, format, anchor_id?,
    fraction, max_fraction, state (started|finished), finished_version?, started_at, finished_at?,
    updated_at`. Unique `(user_id, tenant_id, project, doc)`; index `(user_id, updated_at desc)` and
    `(user_id, tenant_id, project, unit)`.
  - `reader_bookmarks`: `id, user_id, tenant_id, project, doc, unit, lang, version, anchor jsonb, label?,
    snippet, created_at, updated_at`. Index `(user_id, tenant_id, project, unit)`.
  - `reader_annotations`: `id, user_id, tenant_id, project, doc, unit, lang, version, block_id, quote
    jsonb? ({exact, prefix, suffix, start?, end?}), note?, snapshot jsonb ({text, block_title,
    chapter_title}), anchor_status (anchored|block|orphan), anchored_version, created_at, updated_at`.
    Check: `quote` or `note` is present. Index `(user_id, tenant_id, project, unit)`.
  - `quiz_attempts`: `id, user_id, tenant_id, project, doc, unit, lang, version, block_id, item_id,
    item_type, response jsonb, correct, attempt, answered_at, created_at`. Unique `(user_id, tenant_id,
    project, unit, item_id, attempt)`.
  - `review_cards`: `id, user_id, tenant_id, project, unit, source (quiz|highlight), item_id?,
    annotation_id?` (FK `reader_annotations` ON DELETE CASCADE), `item_type, lang, status
    (active|suspended|retired), due, stability, difficulty, fsrs_state, step?, reps, lapses,
    last_review?, created_at, updated_at`. Partial unique `(user_id, tenant_id, project, unit, item_id)
    WHERE source='quiz'`; unique `annotation_id`; index `(user_id, status, due)`.
  - `review_logs`: `id, card_id` (FK ON DELETE CASCADE), `user_id, rating, response jsonb?,
    reviewed_at, state_before, scheduled_days, elapsed_days, duration_ms?`. Append-only; kept for a
    later per-user optimisation.
  - `reader_profiles`: `user_id` (PK, FK CASCADE), `time_zone` (IANA), `day_starts_at_hour` (default 4),
    `max_reviews_per_day` (default 200), `new_highlights_per_day` (default 20), `updated_at`.
  - **Cascades:** account deleted → every row (FK); org deleted → every row on its content (FK);
    **document deleted** → the delete handlers (the `/app` and `/api` routes) purge every user's rows for
    `(tenant, project, doc)` in the same request, because SQLite cannot hold an FK (Operator Question 8);
    **chapter or book removed from a manifest** → no delete: the document is loose, and `unit` is
    re-written to `d:<doc>`; **project turned private, or membership lost** → **nothing is written**:
    rows are kept and hidden by the readability re-check, and they come back if access returns.
- **Anchors (P29.S3).**
  - **The stored anchor** is a W3C-style layered selector inside one block: `{block_id, quote: {exact,
    prefix (≤ 32 chars), suffix (≤ 32 chars)}, start?, end?}`, where `start`/`end` are offsets in the
    block's **plain-text projection** (the S2 search projection, per block). It is captured in the
    browser from the Selection and the rendered block.
  - **Re-anchoring runs on the server, in Python, over the stored source**, in the reader-state API: the
    chapter read returns each annotation resolved for the requested version as `{block_id, start, end,
    status}`, and the client only paints offsets. One implementation; the lists and the review get the
    same statuses without opening the chapter. The S2 conformance fixtures add each block's plain-text
    projection so the TS renderer's text matches Python's offsets — a P30 core test.
  - **The ladder:** (1) `block_id` present → an exact match of `exact` in the block, disambiguated by
    prefix/suffix, then by `start`; (2) a fuzzy match in the block (similarity ≥ 0.8 over the quote with
    its context); (3) an exact match anywhere in the chapter (moved text); (4) the block exists but the
    quote does not → status **`block`**: shown as a margin marker on the block with the original
    snapshot; (5) the id is gone (a retired section) → status **`orphan`**. **Nothing is ever deleted or
    moved automatically.** `anchor_status` and `anchored_version` are written back when they change.
  - **Orphans are shown** in an **Unanchored** group at the end of the chapter's annotation panel and in
    the per-book list, with the original text struck through (Hypothesis's pattern), and can be deleted
    there.
  - **Language switch:** resume and bookmarks map by id (ids are identical across variants); an id
    missing in the other language falls back to the nearest preceding id, else `fraction`. **Highlights
    belong to their language**: in the other language a highlighted block shows a block marker ("1
    highlight in 한국어") that opens the original quote and note; it is never painted onto the
    translation.
  - **Legacy:** resume and progress through the bridge only, and bookmarks as positions; no highlights,
    notes or quiz results. After the `html → native` migration the rows keep their key (the stem
    survives); a legacy `anchor` restores by id if the native chapter carries the same id, else by
    `fraction`, and legacy bookmarks show as **approximate**.
- **Review items (P29.S3).** Every quiz type enters review **automatically** on its first attempt; the
  reader can **suspend** a card. The first attempt is the card's first review: correct (or `got`) →
  **Good**, wrong (or `missed`) → **Again**. A **highlight** becomes a card only when the reader turns on
  **Review this** for it (Operator Question 7): a resurfacing card (the quote, its context and the note)
  that the reader rates.
- **Algorithm: FSRS (P29.S3).** FSRS-6 through **`py-fsrs`** (`fsrs` on PyPI, MIT), run **on the server
  at submission**; `ts-fsrs` (MIT) is not needed, because the server returns the interval previews.
  - **Parameters:** the library defaults; `desired_retention` 0.9; `learning_steps` and
    `relearning_steps` = one 10-minute step (Anki: steps under a day); `maximum_interval` 36500;
    fuzzing on.
  - **No per-user optimisation in v1:** the optimiser is an extra install with a heavy dependency;
    `review_logs` keep what a later optimisation needs.
  - **Rejected:** SM-2 (no retention target, "ease hell", and Anki itself moved to FSRS in 23.10);
    Leitner boxes (simple, but coarse and with no retention model); the client-side scheduler (two
    runtimes and client-trusted state).
- **Ratings: two buttons (P29.S3).** The review surface rates **Again / Good** only; FSRS's Hard and Easy
  are unused. `choice` and `cloze` are re-asked and graded as in the reader (correct → Good, wrong →
  Again: the grade is shown and the reader presses **Next**); `recall` is self-graded `missed` / `got`
  (→ Again / Good); a highlight card asks **Again** ("show sooner") / **Good** ("got it"). Each button
  shows its interval preview.
- **Item drift (P29.S3).** On a new version: an item id that survives keeps its card and renders the new
  content; a surviving id whose `type` changed **resets** the card (logs kept); a retired id makes the
  card **retired** (out of the queue, kept with its logs; ids are never reused). A card renders in the
  reader's last-used language for that unit, else any readable variant. A card whose chapter is
  unreadable to the reader (private, membership lost, deleted) **never appears**: not in the queue, not
  in any count, not in any payload — the same constant 404 as every other miss.
- **Review data contract (P29.S3).**
  - `GET /app/reader/review/queue?limit=` → `{now, day: {start, end, time_zone}, counts: {due,
    new_highlights, done_today, by_book: [{org, project, book?, book_title, due, new}]}, next_due_at?,
    cards: [card]}`. **Due** = `due <= end of today` (local day, starting at `day_starts_at_hour`);
    order: overdue relearning first, then by due. Limits: `max_reviews_per_day`, and
    `new_highlights_per_day` for highlight cards never reviewed (quiz cards are seeded by an answer, so
    they are never "new").
  - **A card:** `{card_id, source, item_type, state, reps, lapses, due, where: {org, project, book?,
    book_title?, chapter_key?, chapter_title, doc, lang, version, href} (href deep-links to the block),
    front, back, previews: {again: {due, interval}, good: {due, interval}}}`, with `front`/`back` per
    type:
    - `choice`: `{prompt, options[{id, text}], multi}` / `{answer[ids], explain}`;
    - `cloze`: `{text with blanks, blanks}` / `{accepted[[…]], explain?}`;
    - `recall`: `{prompt}` / `{answer, explain?}`;
    - highlight: `{quote, block_title, note?}` / none.

    Markdown fields arrive rendered by the native pipeline. The key ships with the card, as in the
    reader (a learning tool).
  - `POST /app/reader/review/cards/{id}/review` with `{rating: again|good, response?, review_id}` (an
    idempotency UUID) → `{card: {due, interval, state}, counts}`. The server's clock decides.
  - `PATCH /app/reader/review/cards/{id}` `{status: active|suspended}`; `PATCH /app/reader/profile`
    `{time_zone, max_reviews_per_day, new_highlights_per_day}`. The time zone is taken from the
    browser's `Intl` on the first write and is editable.
  - **States the surface needs:** cards due, **done for today** (with `next_due_at`), **empty** (no
    cards yet: answer a quiz or mark a highlight).
- **Routes and writes (P29.S3).** FastAPI `require_user` routes under **`/app/reader/…`**, called from
  server components and server actions through `client.ts`:
  - reads: `GET continue?limit=`; `GET library` (per-book progress, batched); `GET
    books/{org}/{project}/{book}` (per-chapter state, resume, book progress, continue pointer); `GET
    chapters/{org}/{project}/{book}/{key}?lang=&v=` and `GET docs/{org}/{project}/{doc}?v=` (position,
    bookmarks, resolved annotations, latest quiz attempts, in one call); `GET annotations?org=&project=
    &book=` (the per-book list, orphans included);
  - writes: `PUT positions`; `POST/PATCH/DELETE bookmarks[/{id}]`; `POST/PATCH/DELETE
    annotations[/{id}]` (`PATCH {review: true}` adds a highlight card); `POST quiz-attempts` → `{attempt,
    card?}`; the review routes above; `DELETE state?scope=book|all`.
  - **One route handler:** `POST /api/reader/position`, for `navigator.sendBeacon` on `pagehide` /
    `visibilitychange: hidden` (a server action cannot be beaconed). It unseals the session and relays.
  - **Cadence:** the position is written 2 s after scrolling stops and at most every 15 s while reading,
    plus the beacon; `finished` and every other write are immediate.
  - **Conflicts (no offline mode, so the server is the only copy):** position = last write wins, except
    that `max_fraction` only grows and `finished` only sets. Mark as unread is the explicit undo, and it is
    the one write that resets `max_fraction` to 0 (amended at P29.S4's sign-off; see *Resume and
    progress*);
    bookmarks and annotations are independent rows (a union), an edit is last-write-wins per row, and a
    delete wins (a later write to a deleted id is a 404); quiz attempts are append-only and the server
    numbers them; reviews are idempotent by `review_id`.
  - **Isolation:** every query carries `user_id = <caller>`; a row id from another user is the constant
    404. Every read and write **re-checks readability** of `(tenant, project, doc)` exactly as
    `_resolve_readable_doc` does (member of the owner tenant, or a public project, and the document
    exists); a miss is the constant 404 and writes nothing.
  - **Compatibility:** new tables and new routes only; the quiz payload ships with `doc` and `unit` from
    the start (the accepted amendment), so no `doc_slug` payload ever exists. **Nothing is breaking.**
- **Privacy (P29.S3).** Reader state is the reader's alone: no route exposes it to the book's owner or to
  org members, there are no aggregates or analytics over it, and it never enters search, embeddings, MCP,
  the graph or `usage_events` (it is free and unmetered). The reader can delete any single bookmark,
  annotation or card, clear one book's state, or clear **all** reader state (`DELETE state`). Losing
  access keeps the rows, hidden; a deleted document purges them (Operator Question 8). Snapshots (quote,
  block and chapter titles) are copies of content and render only after the same readability check.
