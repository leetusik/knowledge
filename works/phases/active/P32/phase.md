- **(from P32.DECOMP, for P32.S1) How research teams are planned, run and stopped** → `topics/research-method.md`. Open at least: Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system" (2025-06-13) and its published lead/subagent prompts (anthropic-cookbook `patterns/agents/prompts/`); OpenAI deep research (launch post, system card, the Deep Research API docs and cookbook: clarify → rewrite → research); Google Gemini Deep Research (its published overview and the editable plan; the TTD-DR "test-time diffusion" paper); Stanford STORM (Shao et al., NAACL 2024) and Co-STORM (Jiang et al., EMNLP 2024); open deep-research frameworks as secondary evidence (LangChain open_deep_research and its write-up, GPT Researcher); how deep research is evaluated (Anthropic's LLM-judge rubric, DeepResearch Bench). Answer:
  1. **Stages:** what the best systems run from request to report (scope/clarify, plan, fan-out, synthesize, cite), and which P32 needs for a single document and for a book.
  2. **The plan:** how the questions the stated reader needs are found and split into angles that do not overlap (STORM perspectives, Co-STORM's mind map, Gemini's plan); for a general subject and for a codebase subject (the intent's angle lists).
  3. **The brief:** what each researcher's brief holds (objective, output format, source and tool guidance, boundaries), task size, how many researchers per question complexity, and what each returns (to a file, not the lead's context).
  4. **Wide then deep:** search strategy, effort budgets, parallel calls; the failure modes the authors report (duplicate work, over-spawning, endless search, SEO-farm sources) and their fixes.
  5. **Gap rounds:** how a coverage round is driven ("what would an expert expect, what would this reader still ask"), what decides the next round, the stopping rule and the cap, and how a capped run says so.
  6. **Research to writing:** how an outline is built from research (STORM), how a writer draws only from collected material, and what the writer is handed when the dossier is large.
  7. **Evaluation:** the rubrics these systems are judged on, as input to P32's old-vs-new rehearsal rubric.
  8. **Recommend:** P32's stages for the single document and the book (whole-book research before the outline, a per-chapter top-up), each agent's role and brief template, round and stop rules, and what `quick` drops. Not S1's: the ledger, verification and the critic (S2); hosts (S3).

Open at least: FActScore (Min et al., 2023); SAFE and LongFact (Wei et al., 2024, "Long-form factuality in large language models"); VeriScore (Song et al., 2024) and work on claim decomposition; ALCE (Gao et al., 2023, citations); AIS (Rashkin et al., attribution); "Evaluating verifiability in generative search engines" (Liu et al., 2023); RARR (research and revise); Chain-of-Verification (Dhuliawala et al., 2023); correctness vs. faithfulness of citations (Wallat et al., 2024); LLM self-preference (Panickssery et al., 2024) as the evidence for a different-model verifier; source-credibility practice (SIFT and lateral reading: Caulfield; Wineburg and McGrew), Wikipedia's reliable-sources and primary-source guidelines as a tested ladder; locally, P31.S5's `result.md` head (the critic's two must-fixes) and `P31/package/claude/agents/explain-critic.md`'s rubric.   3. **Atomic claims:** how claims are split and decontextualized, what works and fails, and which claims of a chapter need ledger entries.
  4. **Verification:** how a verifier re-opens a source and judges claim against passage, what models get wrong doing it, and how independent it must be (a different model, a fresh context, none of the researcher's reasoning).
  5. **Counter-evidence:** how disconfirming evidence is searched for the main claims, and how a disagreement is recorded.
  6. **The critic against the ledger:** checking a chapter claim by claim (an untraceable claim is a finding) and for coverage against the plan's questions; severity and confidence; what P31's critic rubric covers and lacks.
  7. **Loud failure:** the minimum evidence a run needs to proceed (no web research means stop), and what a load-bearing claim with no primary source does to a run.
  8. **Recommend:** the dossier and ledger schema with one worked entry, the ladder, the verifier's procedure and model rule, the counter-evidence pass, the critic's ledger and coverage checks; then one page integrating S1's stages and this into P32's recommended pipeline, single document and book.
